# Entrant 41: Fallline (lens: what-if simulation)

## 0. Bold bet
Fallline lets a buyer break its supply chain on purpose before reality does: pick a supplier, pick a failure (shutdown, recall, sanction, strike), and watch the blast radius ripple through tiers with days-to-stockout, then click a switch and approve the order. Judges get an interactive, numeric answer to "what if?" every 30 seconds of the demo, which is more memorable and more auditable than a static alert.

## 1. Summary and name
**Fallline**: a what-if console for supply-chain risk. A buyer selects a node in its map and a scenario. The simulation engine computes exposed companies, Valio SKUs, recall reach and days to stockout, respecting trust levels. The procurement agent proposes switches to opted-in alternatives, previewed in a sandbox overlay. A manager approves; the order goes through the Valio mock API and every simulation is logged and reproducible.

## 2. Architecture and DB choice
- **Postgres 16 on Aiven** is the system of record: companies, products, supply edges, lots, inventory, scenarios. Hosts the app on Aiven Runtime.
- **In-memory graph engine** (TypeScript, graphology) loads the relevant subgraph (under 5k nodes) on request and runs traversals in milliseconds. This makes sliders feel live, which a DB round-trip per click would not.
- **ArcadeDB**: only as an optional Cypher cross-check of the blast-radius result, run on Saturday if Docker works in ten minutes. Not on the critical path. Assumption: its supply-chain examples run unmodified.
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash** classifies news and writes the narrative from engine JSON; it never produces numbers. A post-check rejects any figure absent from the JSON and falls back to a template.
- **Context.dev** feeds live events via news search and domain monitors with webhooks.

## 3. Data model and key queries
```sql
company(id pk, name, domain, country, industry, status, trust_summary jsonb)
product(id pk, valio_sku, name, category, unit_price, published bool)
supply_edge(id pk, from_id, to_id, trust text check (trust in ('inferred','claimed','agreed','audited')),
  confidence numeric, sources jsonb, unique(from_id,to_id))
product_source(product_id, supplier_id, lead_time_days, price_range numrange, opt_in bool,
  primary key(product_id, supplier_id))
inventory_snapshot(product_id, days_cover numeric, as_of date)       -- synthetic, assumption
lot(id pk, product_id, supplier_id, lot_code, shipped_on date)
lot_delivery(lot_id, buyer_id, qty, delivered_on)                    -- time-series in same table
scenario(id pk, owner_id, base_as_of timestamptz, trust_floor text, horizon_days int)
scenario_change(scenario_id, kind text check (kind in ('fail','recall','sanction','strike','switch')),
  target_id, params jsonb)
```
- **Trust floor** is a scenario parameter: the engine only follows edges at or above it. Inferred edges count only with confidence ≥ 0.6 and at least one source URL.
- **Privacy** is enforced at the API: a buyer's scenario may read only its own subgraph plus risk-level outputs and opt-in alternatives. Valio's operator view sees all.
- **Blast radius** (recursive, downstream, depth ≤ 4):
```sql
WITH RECURSIVE down(node, depth, path) AS (
  SELECT to_id, 1, ARRAY[from_id,to_id] FROM supply_edge
   WHERE from_id = $failed AND trust_rank >= $floor
  UNION ALL
  SELECT e.to_id, d.depth+1, d.path||e.to_id FROM down d
   JOIN supply_edge e ON e.from_id = d.node
   WHERE d.depth < 4 AND NOT e.to_id = ANY(d.path) AND e.trust_rank >= $floor)
SELECT node, min(depth) AS hops FROM down GROUP BY node;
```
- **Exposed SKUs and days to stockout**: join `product_source` on the failed supplier and its downstream set, then `days_cover - lead_time_days` for the time-to-impact.
- **Recall reach**: `SELECT buyer_id, sum(qty) FROM lot_delivery WHERE lot_id IN (SELECT id FROM lot WHERE supplier_id IN (...) AND shipped_on >= $since) GROUP BY buyer_id;`
- **Concentration** (hidden shared upstream): count of direct suppliers of a buyer that have an edge from the same upstream node, ≥ 2.
- **Alternatives**: `product_source` rows with `opt_in = true` for the same product, with price range and lead-time delta against the failed source.

## 4. Agents
- **Discovery** (live web): for every supplier in a scenario, Context.dev news search over the last 30 days, returning headlines, dates and URLs. Gemini classifies each item into a risk type with a severity, and the item is stored with its source URL. Items with no URL are discarded. Discovered upstream edges enter as `inferred` with confidence; they never change exposure without the trust floor.
- **Monitoring**: Context.dev monitors on supplier domains and news queries, delivering webhooks to `/hooks/ctx`. A hit creates a *suggested scenario* ("Live event: plant shutdown reported at X, source URL") that a human must click to simulate. A scripted trigger replays one recorded event for reliability.
- **Procurement**: for each exposed SKU, picks the best opted-in alternative by coverage, then price delta, then lead-time delta. Produces a switch proposal. Orders are drafted only; placement requires manager approval in the UI, then the Valio mock order API. Each step is written to the audit log with the scenario hash.

## 5. Risk engine
Simulation is the risk engine. Each risk type maps to a scenario kind or signal:
- **Geopolitical and sanctions**: `sanction` scenario on a country or node; seeded sanctions list plus news.
- **Compliance**: expired or missing certificate raises a warning on the supplier; unknown dates are flagged.
- **Concentration**: hidden-concentration query ("3 suppliers rely on one farm").
- **Financial, live events, price premium**: Gemini classifies news (layoffs, insolvency, recall, strike); premium is current price above the published range.
- **Score** for a scenario: exposed revenue share × hop-decay (0.7 per tier) × confidence, plus days-to-stockout penalty.

## 6. Screens and graph view
1. **What-if console** (hero): graph on the left (Valio centre for the operator; buyer's own map for the hotel). Right panel: event type, target node, trust-floor slider (inferred / claimed / agreed / audited), horizon slider (days). KPIs update live: companies exposed, SKUs exposed, days to first stockout, alternatives coverage.
2. **Cascade view**: press play and the failure ripples outward by tier at one second per hop. Nodes colour by days to impact (red under 3 days, amber under 10, grey beyond). Edge styles follow trust (dashed inferred, dotted claimed, solid agreed, double audited).
3. **Switch planner**: table per exposed SKU: current source, best alternative (opt-in only), price delta, lead-time delta, trust level. "Apply in sandbox" draws the new edges as an overlay and recomputes KPIs without touching real data.
4. **Recall tracer**: enter a lot code; shows buyers reached (operator view) or the hotel's own deliveries only.
5. **Approvals and history**: drafted switches, approve button, Valio mock order result, and each run with its inputs hash so re-runs give identical numbers.

## 7. Demo script (4 min)
- **0:00** Live console with Valio at centre. A card appears: "Live event: Context.dev monitor found a plant shutdown notice at Maitokorpi Oy (fictional), source URL." Click "Simulate".
- **0:40** Cascade plays: Maitokorpi → Pohjola Dairy Co-op (fictional) → Kestrel Hotels (fictional) and 13 other buyers. KPI: 6 SKUs exposed, 9 days to stockout.
- **1:30** Move the trust slider from inferred to agreed: exposure drops from 14 buyers to 9. "Trust changes the answer, and we show why."
- **2:00** Recall tracer on lot code: 212 Valio customers reached; the hotel view shows only its own 3 deliveries.
- **2:30** Switch planner: 4 of 6 SKUs have opted-in alternatives; +4% price, +1 day lead time; apply in sandbox; KPIs recompute.
- **3:10** Gemini narrative, number-checked, cites the source URL. Manager approves; Valio mock order placed; audit log shows scenario hash.
- **3:40** Auditor stamps the Pohjola edge `audited`; rerun: exposure falls because the edge now passes the trust floor at high confidence.
- **3:55** Close: "Every number here is reproducible from the logged inputs."

## 9. Judging-criteria mapping
- **Valio (30%)**: end-to-end from event to approved order; guardrails (draft-only, approval gate, audit log with reproducible inputs); exception handling via partial availability and substitutions in the switch planner.
- **Feasibility (25%)**: one database plus an in-memory engine; no new infrastructure; trigger replays for reliability.
- **Context.dev (15%)**: news search in the scenario path, monitors that create live scenarios, cited URLs.
- **Trust and privacy (10%)**: the trust floor is a visible control; buyer views are limited to its own subgraph.
- **Side bounties (10%)**: Gemini for classification and narrative; Aiven Postgres and Runtime; CRACI in CI. Cala skipped.

## 10. Risks and cuts
- **Cut order**: ArcadeDB cross-check → Gemini narrative (template) → live monitor (scripted trigger) → sandbox overlay (table only).
- **Risks**: synthetic inventory looks unrealistic (label as synthetic); Context.dev latency (pre-cache domains); slider bugs (unit-test three demo scenarios with fixed expected numbers).

## 11. Biggest weakness of this proposal
The numbers depend on invented inventory cover and lead-time fields. If the Valio synthetic data lacks a usable product-to-supplier mapping, the stockout and exposure figures become hand-tuned, and a sharp judge may notice. Onboarding and network growth get little attention.
