# Entrant 42: Every Risk Has a Euro Sign (lens: Money story)

## 0. Bold bet
The buyer's hook is one number in euros: what they overpay versus visible, opted-in alternatives, net of the risk they would take on by switching. Every alert, graph edge and agent proposal is reduced to that figure, so judges see value in seconds and the procurement agent acts only on money it can prove.

## 1. Summary and name
- **Pennyline** (working name). A hotel buyer logs in and sees "€41,870 overpaid last 12 months, €29,400 net of risk, €29,400 recoverable with one approved switch." The supply network explains where the money and the risk sit. Price premiums are computed from published prices and order history, not from opinion.
- Assumptions: Valio mock API provides a 12-month order history for the demo hotel and fixed prices per product. Dummy numbers below are illustrative and seeded.

## 2. Architecture and DB choice
- Decision: **Aiven Postgres as the single store**. Reasons: the money story is SQL aggregation (window functions, percentiles, price history) and Postgres does it natively. Recursive CTEs cover four-tier upstream traversal. pgvector covers alternative similarity (assumed available on Aiven). One database means no sync code and no second operational surface.
- ArcadeDB is a labelled stretch: if the Cypher spike works by Saturday noon, blast-radius queries move there. Otherwise it is cut.
- Components: React app; Fastify API; Postgres (Aiven); job runner in the API process; Gemini function calling for agents; Context.dev for extraction and monitors; Valio mock client. Aiven Runtime hosts the API if time allows.
- Flow: every money number is computed in SQL. The LLM writes words, never arithmetic.

## 3. Data model and key queries
- `companies(id, name, country, industry, status, risk_score)`
- `edges(id, supplier_id, buyer_id, trust, confidence, sources jsonb, owner_id)`; trust is an enum of `inferred|claimed|agreed|audited`, with a CHECK constraint.
- `products(id, gtin, category, base_unit, base_qty)`. Prices normalise to price per base unit (per kg or per l).
- `price_points(product_id, seller_id, price_per_base, published bool, source_url, observed_at)`. Only `published = true` rows feed comparisons.
- `order_lines(order_id, buyer_id, product_id, seller_id, qty_base, unit_price_per_base, ordered_at)`
- `risk_signals`, `alerts`, `orders`, `approvals(role, user_id, decided_at)`, `policy(max_switch_eur, min_net_saving_eur)`
- `audit_event(prev_hash, hash, actor, action, payload)`; append-only trigger.
- Visibility: alternatives come only where `listing_consent.opted_in`. Buyer views never expose another company's `edges`; they see alerts, aggregates (k of at least 5) and published prices.
- Premium query (last 365 days):
  ```
  WITH best AS (SELECT pp.product_id, min(pp.price_per_base) best
    FROM price_points pp JOIN listing_consent lc USING (seller_id, product_id)
    WHERE pp.published AND lc.opted_in GROUP BY pp.product_id)
  SELECT ol.product_id, sum((ol.unit_price_per_base - b.best) * ol.qty_base) premium_eur
  FROM order_lines ol JOIN best b USING (product_id)
  WHERE ol.buyer_id = $buyer AND ol.ordered_at > now() - interval '365 days'
    AND ol.unit_price_per_base > b.best
  GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 2 DESC;
  ```
- Hidden concentration: recursive CTE up to 4 tiers, then `GROUP BY node HAVING count(DISTINCT direct) >= 2`.

## 4. Agents (discovery, monitoring, procurement)
- **Discovery.** Input: public catalogue and price-page URLs. Tools: Context.dev `web.extract` (structured JSON with price, unit, currency), `company.enrich`. Output: `price_points` with `published = true` only for public pages, plus `inferred` edges. Validation: a price needs a URL, a unit and a parseable currency. Match confidence on product: GTIN equals 1.0; category plus unit match equals 0.7 (assumption, labelled on screen).
- **Monitoring.** Context.dev monitors on price and recall pages, with webhook and a 15-minute poll fallback. A price change triggers premium recompute. Output: `alerts` with money impact and source URL.
- **Procurement.** Tools: `alternatives(product)` (pgvector candidates, opted-in filter), `net_saving(...)`, `draft_order` against Valio fixed prices. Output: proposal with unit price, lead-time delta, risk delta, net saving. Guardrails in code: `place_order` requires an `approvals` row, and the amount must sit under `policy.max_switch_eur`. Proposals below `min_net_saving_eur` are not created.

## 5. Risk engine
- Each risk becomes money: expected annual loss = annual spend × p(disruption) × 15% margin loss (assumption).
- p(disruption) = 1 − exp(−0.8 × risk_score), with risk_score 0 to 1 (assumption; tuned on seeded data).
- Inputs: geopolitical (country table, dummy), regulatory (sanctions name match, certificate expiry from Valio sustainability data, EUDR/CSDDD flags), concentration (single-source; shared upstream from the CTE), financial (keyword and LLM classification of news), live events (monitor hits), price premium (section 3 query).
- Net saving = premium recoverable − (expected loss of alternative − expected loss of current). A switch only shows if net saving is positive.
- Upstream propagation: risk on a tier-n supplier counts at 0.8^n.

## 6. Screens and graph view
- **Money dashboard (hero):** three tiles: overpaid 12m, net of risk, recoverable with approval. Bar chart of premium by category. Each tile links to its calculation.
- **Supply map:** nodes sized by spend; edges styled by trust. Risky nodes coloured; hover shows euro exposure.
- **Alert card:** price change, € impact, sources with timestamps.
- **Switch proposal:** current vs two alternatives side by side: unit price, delivery days, risk score, net saving. Agent trace below. Approve button.
- **Order and ledger:** Valio mock order ID, approval, audit hash verified.
- **Supplier and auditor views:** publish prices; confirm edge; stamp `audited`.

## 7. Demo script (about 4 minutes)
- 0:00 Hook: "Your hotel overpaid €41,870 last year. Here is why, and what it costs to fix."
- 0:25 Dashboard tiles. Click premium: dairy leads at €18,200.
- 0:55 Live beat: Context.dev extracts a public dairy price page. Price change appears with timestamp and URL. Premium recomputes on screen.
- 1:30 Supply map: upstream dairy producer carries a risk flag; euro exposure €11,300.
- 1:55 Switch proposal: two opted-in alternatives, net saving €29,400 after risk. Gemini explains; numbers come from SQL.
- 2:35 Supplier confirms edge `claimed` to `agreed`, live.
- 3:00 Manager approves. Valio mock order placed. Partial availability triggers substitution at the same price.
- 3:30 Ledger: audit chain verified. Privacy beat: competitor map hidden; "used by 7 hotels in region" aggregate.
- 3:55 Auditor stamps `audited`. Close on the number.

## 9. Judging-criteria mapping
- Valio (30%): fixed-price order via mock API, policy and approval gates, ledger.
- Buyer need (20%): one euro figure, net of risk, with recoverable savings.
- Exceptions (15%): partial availability to substitution at the same price.
- Open standards (15%): Postgres, OpenAPI, SQL reporting.
- Context.dev (15%): live price extraction and monitor, URLs on screen.
- Trust and privacy (10%): trust enum, opt-in filter, k of at least 5.
- Bounties: Gemini (agent LLM), Aiven (Postgres, Runtime), Codex (dev), CRACI (CI). Cala excluded.

## 10. Risks and cuts
- Cut first: ArcadeDB stretch, then the supply map animation, then Oxylabs and Modal.
- Biggest risk: product matching across suppliers. Mitigation: GTIN where it exists, a labelled 0.7 confidence otherwise, and only high-confidence matches shown.
- Second risk: premium looks small if few prices are published. Mitigation: seeded hotel history gives meaningful spend, and only published prices count.
- Context.dev latency: pre-cached live response with timestamp.

## 11. Biggest weakness of this proposal
The savings figure depends on seeded order history and on how many suppliers publish prices. A judge who asks "how many real prices did you actually compare?" will find a thin answer. Also, one Postgres store gives up graph-native queries, so the network story is weaker than a graph-first entry.
