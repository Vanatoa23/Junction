# Entrant 63: Undergrowth, a Weather Forecast for Your Food (lens: Wildcard)

## 0. Bold bet
Stop showing buyers a graph and a procurement dashboard. Draw the supply chain as roots under a sky, with risks as storms that drift up the roots toward the kitchen, and let the buyer steer by asking questions in plain language. The judge gets the whole idea in 20 seconds, and every answer on screen carries a source URL and a trust level.

## 1. Summary and name
- **Undergrowth** (working name). A hotel kitchen manager opens a forecast: storms over suppliers, roots connecting them to the kitchen. Storms are risks (recalls, sanctions hits, price spikes, shared upstream dependence). Roots are supplier edges, styled by trust. The manager asks "what's coming for our milk?" and the Forecast agent answers with a map, one sentence and citations. Switching and ordering stay formal, with a human seal before any order.
- Assumptions: text chat is the baseline; voice is a cut. Valio mock API supplies the hotel's order history, fixed prices and availability.

## 2. Architecture and DB choice
- **ArcadeDB as the single store.** The metaphor needs traversal (roots), time-series (weather readings over 90 days), vectors (alternatives) and JSON (certificates) in one place. One engine avoids sync code in a 48-hour build.
- **Aiven Postgres** holds users, orders, approvals and the append-only audit log. Legal records are separated from the network, and this uses the Aiven bounty honestly. The API joins across the two in code, only on the order screen.
- Components: React app with an SVG root map; Fastify API; Forecast agent (Gemini function calling); Context.dev monitors as "weather stations" with webhooks; Valio mock client.
- Fallback: the same schema can run in Postgres with recursive CTEs if the ArcadeDB Docker setup fails on the day. This is assumed, not yet spiked.

## 3. Data model and key queries
- Vertices: `Company {id, name, country, industry, status}`, `Product {id, category, embedding}`, `Storm {id, type, intensity, source_urls[], status, first_seen}`, `Reading {metric, value, ts}` (time-series).
- Edges: `ROOT` (supplier to buyer) `{trust in [inferred, claimed, agreed, audited], confidence, sources[], owner_id}`; `AFFECTS` (Storm to Company); `OFFERS` (Company to Product).
- Visibility: buyers see storms, aggregates (k of at least 5) and opted-in alternatives. Other companies' `ROOT` edges are never returned. The buyer sees only "N unseen roots" for hidden upstream.
- Storm drift (storm over a supplier reaching the buyer):
  ```
  MATCH (s:Storm {id:$storm})-[:AFFECTS]->(c:Company)-[:ROOT*1..3]->(b:Company {id:$buyer})
  RETURN c.id, b.id, length(...)
  ```
- Upstream roots: `MATCH p=(u:Company)-[:ROOT*1..4]->(b:Company {id:$buyer}) WHERE ALL(e IN relationships(p) WHERE e.confidence >= $minConf) RETURN u.id, length(p)`.
- Convergence (hidden concentration): the same upstream company reached by two or more direct suppliers, using `count(DISTINCT direct)`.
- Blast radius: `MATCH (f:Company {id:$failed})-[:ROOT*1..3]->(c:Company) RETURN c.id`.
- Alternatives: vector neighbours on product embeddings (labelled assumption on syntax), filtered by an `optedIn` property and joined to published price documents.

## 4. Agents (discovery, monitoring, procurement)
- **Weather stations (discovery).** Context.dev crawls supplier and news pages and enriches companies. Output: `ROOT` edges as `inferred` with confidence and URL. A `Storm` candidate is created only when a news item has a URL. Validation: no URL, no edge.
- **Monitoring.** Context.dev monitors send webhooks on page change. A recall or price change updates the `Storm` intensity and appends a `Reading`. A 15-minute poll is the fallback.
- **Forecast agent (procurement).** Gemini with tools: `forecast(buyer)`, `alternatives(product)`, `draft_switch`, `request_approval`, `place_order`. Every claim in the reply cites a storm or source ID. `place_order` is refused in the API unless an approval row exists for that proposal.

## 5. Risk engine
- Storm intensity = 1 − product of (1 − weight) over signals from the last 90 days. Weights: sanctions match 1.0, recall 0.8, certificate expiry 0.5, negative news 0.3, country table 0.2 (all assumptions, tuned on seeds).
- Price storm: intensity from premium over the cheapest opted-in published price, as a share of spend.
- Drift: a storm at tier n reaches the kitchen with intensity × 0.8^n.
- Convergence: a storm reaching the buyer by two or more paths gets a distinct flag. This is the most alarming symbol on the map.

## 6. Screens and graph view
- **Forecast (home):** sky over the kitchen, storms over suppliers, one sentence per storm, a 7-day outlook from `Reading`.
- **Roots:** underground map. Thick solid roots for `agreed`, thin dashed for `inferred`, a sealed ring for `audited`. Storms drift along roots as animated arrows toward the kitchen. Nodes not in the buyer's visibility are blank.
- **Ask:** chat box returning answer cards with citations.
- **Switch card:** alternatives with price ranges, lead time, risk, Approve button.
- **Ledger:** orders, approvals, hash-chain check.
- **Supplier:** "sign the root" button for `claimed` to `agreed`. **Auditor:** "seal" button for `audited`.

## 7. Demo script (about 4 minutes)
- 0:00 Forecast sky: "Storm over your milk."
- 0:25 Ask "what's coming for our milk?" Answer card with URL.
- 0:55 Live beat: "Find roots" runs Context.dev on a public supplier page. A new root grows, labelled `inferred` at 0.7, with its URL.
- 1:30 Monitor webhook fires on a dummy producer's recall page. A storm forms and drifts up the roots to the kitchen.
- 2:00 Convergence flag: two roots share an upstream. Alternatives card shows opted-in suppliers with price ranges.
- 2:30 Supplier signs root: `claimed` to `agreed`, live.
- 2:55 Forecast drafts a switch. Manager seals approval. Valio mock order placed.
- 3:25 Partial availability: substitution offered at the same price.
- 3:45 Ledger: hash chain verified. Competitor roots hidden; "used by 7 hotels in region" aggregate.
- 4:00 Auditor seals a root `audited`. Close on the sky.

## 8. 48h build plan (4 engineers, 4 milestones)

- Sat: storm intensity, drift and convergence queries (A, B). Context.dev station writing roots (C). SVG root map with storms (D).
- Sat night: approval gate, Valio mock order, hash-chained audit (B). Monitor webhook and storm animation (C). Switch card and ledger (D).
- Sun, freeze 09:00: lock dummy data, record backup video, rehearse three times, pre-cache the live page.
- Codex used in development with commit evidence. CRACI added to CI.

## 9. Judging-criteria mapping
- Valio (30%): fixed-price order, approval gate in code, ledger.
- Buyer need (20%): plain-language answers with a clear next action.
- Exceptions (15%): partial availability, substitution at the same price.
- Open standards (15%): openCypher, OpenAPI, Postgres for records.
- Context.dev (15%): live crawl and monitor webhook with visible URLs.
- Trust and privacy (10%): trust on every edge, hidden roots, k of at least 5.
- Bounties: Gemini, Aiven, Codex, CRACI. Cala excluded.

## 10. Risks and cuts
- Cut first: voice, storm animation polish, then Oxylabs and Modal.
- Biggest risk: ArcadeDB setup and Cypher dialect. Mitigation: day-one spike, Postgres fallback.
- Canvas drawing time: keep the SVG layout static with simple tweens.
- Context.dev latency: cache the live response and show its timestamp.

## 11. Biggest weakness of this proposal
The metaphor may read as a gimmick to a procurement judge, and storm intensity numbers are arbitrary. Chat-first interaction is less direct than a table for a manager who wants to compare prices. Building an animated root map in 48 hours is a time sink that could eat the approval and order work if it slips.
