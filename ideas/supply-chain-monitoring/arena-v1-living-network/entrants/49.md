# Entrant 49: Dedupe Gate, one legal entity per node (lens: Entity resolution)

## 0. Bold bet
Every candidate company passes through one resolver before it can become a node, so the graph grows by legal entity, not by spelling. The demo shows duplicates being refused live, and the risk and concentration numbers stay correct because they count entities.

## 1. Summary and name
- **Dedupe Gate**: a supply-chain network where every declared, scraped or agent-discovered company is resolved against the existing graph. Hard identifiers (VAT, Finnish Y-tunnus, LEI, domain) decide most matches. Fuzzy names only suggest. Uncertain cases go to a human queue. Merges are logged and reversible.
- Why it matters: a supplier split into two nodes hides shared upstream dependencies (the concentration risk the brief asks for) and doubles risk signals. Resolution is the precondition for the other features.
- **Assumptions:** A1 Valio supplier records carry a business ID or VAT (synthetic data). A2 Context.dev company enrichment returns a canonical domain and country. A3 Finnish business register (PRH) lookup is reachable for Y-tunnus checks, or we use a cached extract of a few hundred companies.

## 2. Architecture and DB choice
- **Postgres on Aiven** with `pg_trgm` for fuzzy name blocking (**A4**: extension available). ArcadeDB is not needed: resolution is a relational problem (identifier uniqueness, merge transactions, blocking indexes), and recursive CTEs cover traversal.
- Components: Node/TypeScript API; a `resolve()` module shared by discovery, monitoring and onboarding; a Gemini extraction step that turns raw names and pages into structured candidates; React UI with Cytoscape.js; the review queue screen.
- Flow: candidate (declared, scraped or enriched) → normalise → block → score → decision (`merge | new | review | reject`) → write `company`, `company_identifier`, edges → `audit_event`.
- Every resolver call is a tool the agents must use. Direct inserts into `company` are blocked at the DB level (write role for the resolver only).

## 3. Data model and key queries
- `company(id, canonical_name, country, status: unregistered|registered, primary_domain, created_at)`
- `company_identifier(company_id, kind: vat|ytunnus|lei|domain|name_norm, value_norm, verified bool, source_url, unique(kind, value_norm) where kind in (vat, ytunnus, lei))`
- `company_alias(company_id, raw_name, source_url, first_seen)`
- `resolution_decision(id, candidate jsonb, matched_company_id, score, decision, reasons jsonb, decided_by: auto|human, at)`
- `merge_log(id, survivor_id, merged_id, decision_id, undone_at)`
- `supply_edge` as usual: `trust`, `confidence`, `sources`, confirmation fields.
- **Normalisation:** lowercase; transliterate ä→a, ö→o, å→a for matching only; strip legal forms (Oy, Oyj, Ab, Ltd, GmbH, AS, SIA); strip punctuation; keep the raw name as an alias.
- **Identifier rules:** Finnish VAT `FI` + 8 digits maps to Y-tunnus `NNNNNNN-C`. The check digit uses weights 7,9,10,5,8,4,2 over the 7 digits; remainder 0 gives check 0, remainder 1 is invalid, otherwise check = 11 − remainder. Domains: strip `www`, use the registrable domain, and ignore webmail domains.
- **Blocking query:** candidates = companies sharing any verified ID or primary domain, or `similarity(name_norm, $n) > 0.45 AND country = $c`, limited to 20.
- **Hard negative:** if the candidate and an existing company both have verified, different VAT/Y-tunnus values, they are never merged, whatever the name.
- **Merge (one transaction):** `UPDATE supply_edge SET from_company=$surv WHERE from_company=$gone;` (same for `to_company`), then collapse duplicate `(from, to, sku_cat)` rows: keep the highest trust, union `sources`. A merge never raises trust to `agreed` or `audited`; those still need confirmations.
- **Visibility:** aliases and identifiers follow the company's visibility. A buyer sees a supplier's canonical name and risk, not another buyer's private raw spellings.

## 4. Agents (discovery, monitoring, procurement)
- **Discovery.** Inputs: Valio supplier list, news and web hits. Tools: `ctx.company_enrich(domain)`, `ctx.news_search(q)`, `ctx.extract(url, schema)`, `resolve(candidate)`. Output: `inferred` edges to resolved companies. Rule: no source URL, no edge. Resolver result `review` creates an `inferred` edge flagged "possible duplicate" and does not create a node.
- **Monitoring.** Context.dev monitors on resolved domains and news queries push webhooks (tunnel; polling fallback every 60 s). Signals attach to the canonical company, so one recall produces one signal across every alias. Confidence below 0.5 is shown but does not trigger procurement.
- **Procurement.** Triggered by a signal at severity ≥70 or a premium alert. Tools: `valio.search`, `valio.availability`, `valio.quote` (fixed price, 30-minute TTL), `graph.alternatives`, `graph.upstream`, `mandate.check`, `propose`. It has no order tool. At most 8 calls per run. It skips alternatives with `review` duplicates and marks them.
- **Exceptions:** partial availability → split order; price moved → re-quote and re-approve if premium rises; delivery slot missed → next slot. Human approval is required for every order. The approval records the proposal payload hash.

## 5. Risk engine
- Score per entity = base × confidence × recency decay (half-life 30 days). Upstream risk passes down with 0.8 per hop.
- **Concentration** is the main resolution payoff: count distinct canonical companies per category. If two direct suppliers share an upstream entity after resolution, the shared-upstream finding appears. Before resolution, the same supplier under two names hides it.
- **Regulatory:** offline EU sanctions consolidated list, matched on name plus the resolver's identifiers (exact ID match outranks name match). EUDR and CSDDD checks run on the canonical company's domain.
- **Geopolitical, financial, live events:** news search and monitors on the canonical company, with country table for geopolitical risk.
- **Price premium:** only from published prices on canonical companies.

## 6. Screens and graph view
1. **Kitchen view** (buyer home): alerts, pending approvals, duplicates prevented this week.
2. **Entity inbox:** review queue for `review` decisions. Side-by-side: candidate vs existing company, shared identifiers, score reasons, source URLs. Buttons: Same entity (merge), Different entity (writes a negative pair), Ask again later.
3. **Company page:** canonical name, aliases with source URLs, identifiers with verified badges, merge history with Undo.
4. **Supply map:** graph view.
5. **Proposal review** and **Audit timeline** (includes every resolver decision).
6. **Supplier portal:** confirm edge, publish prices, opt into alternatives.
7. **Auditor page:** stamp edge `audited` (simulated account).
- **Graph view:** buyer on the right, supply flows left to right. Node label = canonical name; a small "×3" badge shows alias count. Colour = max risk. Dashed border = unregistered. Edge style = trust (dotted inferred, thin claimed, thick agreed, double audited). Edge label = confidence. Possible duplicates show as a dashed connector to the review queue, not as a second node.

## 7. Demo script (4 min)
- **0:00–0:20** Hotel manager logs in. Supply map: Valio Aimo centre, a dairy co-op upstream.
- **0:20–0:50** Seed duplicate: Valio declares "Virtanen Maitotalo Oy" with VAT FI-id; the scraped page says "Maitotalo Virtanen". Dedupe Gate merges them automatically; the audit line shows "VAT match, score 1.00, merged".
- **0:50–1:10** Near-miss: "Virtanen Dairy" shares the domain but has a different VAT. Resolver refuses to merge: "different legal entity."
- **1:10–1:40 LIVE.** "Run discovery and monitors": Context.dev pulls fresh news and a recall notice for the co-op. The new mention resolves onto the existing node with three clickable source URLs. Counter: "duplicates prevented: 3."
- **1:40–2:10** Risk signal on the co-op → procurement proposal with two alternatives. One alternative is a fuzzy match sent to review; the agent excludes it until a human decides. Show the entity inbox, click "Same entity," and the alternative returns.
- **2:10–2:50** Exception: partial availability, split order. Two-approver-free single approval for the hotel's limit. Order placed via Valio mock. Hash chain verified.
- **2:50–3:20** Supplier confirms edge (claimed → agreed). Auditor stamps audited.
- **3:20–3:45** Privacy: aggregate "used by 7 of 40 industry peers"; no names.
- **3:45–4:00** Close: Context.dev in the loop, Gemini, Aiven.

## 9. Judging-criteria mapping
- **Valio (30%):** clean supplier identity means the procurement agent compares real alternatives, not duplicate names. Audit records every resolver decision.
- **Autonomy and controls (20%):** merge rules are deterministic and logged, humans decide uncertain cases, reversible merges.
- **Exceptions (15%):** duplicate suppliers, conflicting identifiers and partial availability all have explicit paths.
- **Context.dev (15%):** enrichment and monitors resolved to canonical IDs; live URLs; real-timeness shown by a new mention landing on an existing node.
- **Trust and privacy (10%):** merges never raise trust; aliases follow company visibility.
- **Concreteness (10%):** normalisation rules, check-digit rule, merge SQL.
- **Side bounties (10%):** Gemini for extraction and classification, Aiven Postgres, CRACI CI pipeline, Codex only if used and logged.

## 10. Risks and cuts
- A1 identifiers missing from Valio data: fall back to domain plus name, which caps auto-merge at review.
- Enrichment returns no domain: resolver keeps the candidate as `review`, and the demo path uses seeded domains.
- Name-normalisation bugs cause wrong merges: the hard-negative rule and "no auto-merge on name alone" limit damage.
- Registry lookups slow or blocked: cached extract.

## 11. Biggest weakness of this proposal
The resolver is the least visible feature, so judges may see a plumbing layer rather than a product. Its demo value depends on planted duplicates, which judges may read as staged. The procurement story is also thinner than in proposals that spend the time on ordering flows.
