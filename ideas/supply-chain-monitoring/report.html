Junction 2026 · Context.dev challenge · 16-entrant arena

Build SecondSource: live supply-chain monitoring that only suggests alternatives the same incident can't touch

16 independent design proposals competed in a knockout bracket, judged against the Context.dev criteria. The winner treats monitoring as the feeder and makes the verified alternative the memorable moment. From the runner-up it takes the on-stage "watch the clock stop" live detection.

The recommendation in one paragraph

The client pastes its supplier list. Cala, Exa and Context.dev turn it into a resolved, enriched and monitored graph in about a minute. Every supplier, and every port and region it depends on, is watched by Context.dev monitors with webhooks. When a change becomes an incident, the incident gets a typed footprint: the regions, ports, plants, sanction programmes and parent companies it touches. An agent then sources replacement suppliers and runs five explicit checks plus a live news check on each one. Only a candidate that passes every row, with a source URL behind each cell, is offered. Anything unknown is shown as "unverified", never as safe. A human approves.

1. Why this design won

  • It matches your actual offer. "When something breaks, here is a supplier the same event cannot hit" is the part nobody else does. The finalist made that rule visible and checkable, cell by cell.
  • It uses Context.dev in three places, not one:
    • monitors and webhooks detect the incident;
    • enrichment and structured extraction build every footprint;
    • a fresh news search vets every alternative before it is offered.
    That last use is the "creative use of live web data" judges rarely see.
  • It is honest by construction.
    • Code drops any claim without a source URL, so the LLM cannot invent evidence.
    • "Unknown" never turns into "pass".
    • Searches sent to Exa, Cala and Context.dev never include the client's name or supplier list, which keeps the privacy rule.
  • It is buildable. Every one of the 16 entrants independently chose Postgres over a graph database and no Kafka. That consensus is the safest call for about 40 hours of build time.

2. How it works

Paste list→Resolve (Cala · Exa · Context.dev)→Footprint extraction→Monitors armed→Webhook→Gemini classify + quote check→Corroborate→Incident footprint→Alternatives agent→5 checks + live news→Human approves
  • One TypeScript service plus one worker on Aiven Runtime. Runtime gives a public URL, so Context.dev webhooks reach us without a tunnel.
  • Aiven Postgres holds everything, including the job queue (FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED). The supply graph is an edge table; recursive queries cover the 2 hops we need.
  • Gemini only classifies and extracts. It must return a verbatim quote, which code checks against the fetched page. The pass/fail checks are plain, unit-tested code, never the LLM.
  • Privacy: public facts (companies, footprints, incidents) are shared and cached. Supply edges, alerts and alternatives carry a client_id and are locked down with Postgres row-level security. A second client login in the demo proves it.

3. Your data bootstrap, with Exa and Cala

You wanted Exa and Cala to put the first companies on the graph. The arena converged on a clear division of labour:

SourceRoleWhat it gives us
CalaIdentity and ownership spineA canonical company ID, legal name, parent group and investors. This feeds the parent and upstream checks, so two "different" suppliers owned by the same group are caught.
ExaDiscoveryFinds the domain for a bare name. Websets and company search build the pool of alternative candidates, by category and outside the incident region.
Context.devThe live reader (core)Enrichment (domain, HQ), structured extraction of plants and ports from "locations" pages, monitors with webhooks, news search.

Resolver: each name is scored on name similarity (0.5), country (0.2) and whether the three sources agree on the domain (0.3).

  • A score of 0.85 or more is accepted automatically.
  • A score between 0.6 and 0.85 shows a one-click chooser. That is a nice live moment where a judge resolves an ambiguity.
  • Anything lower is flagged and asks for a domain.

Target: 10 pasted names fully monitored in under 60 seconds, live. The 40-supplier demo client is pre-loaded and labelled SEEDED.

Note on Cala's prize: its challenge requires a founder or investor need. Here Cala is a data source, so don't expect to win its track without a separate investor story, which split the demo in every proposal that tried it.

4. The incident model and the "same incident" rule

An incident and every company carry a footprint: a set of typed keys, each with a source URL and a state of CONFIRMED, INFERRED or UNKNOWN.

  • Key formats: REGION:FI-UUS, PORT:NLRTM, PLANT:<id>, SANCTION:<programme>, PARENT:<cala id>.
  • Regions are nested. A strike in FI also covers FI-UUS.
  • Names the system can't match block the alert. If an incident names a company or place we cannot resolve, it does not alert automatically; a person maps it first.

The check every alternative must pass

CheckRuleExample result
C1 Regionno shared region, including parent and child regionsPASS SE-O vs FI-UUS
C2 Portno shared port, including inbound lanesFAIL both ship via NLRTM
C3 Upstream plantthe candidate's own tier-2 suppliers don't touch the incident's plantsPASS
C4 Sanctionneither the candidate nor its parent is on the incident's sanctions programmePASS
C5 Parentno shared owner within 2 hops (Cala)UNKNOWN no ownership data
Live checka fresh Context.dev news search on the candidate's region shows no sign of the same incident type in the last 14 daysCLEAN

All checks PASS: offered as verified. Any FAIL: shown greyed out with the failing row. Any UNKNOWN: unverified, only a human can use it, and it never ranks above a verified candidate. Verified candidates are ranked by capacity fit, product match, sourced lead time and a quiet track record. The top 3 are shown, each card cell linked to its evidence.

5. Monitoring design

  • Per supplier:
    • a Context.dev page monitor on /news, /press and its status or locations page, with a webhook on change;
    • a Context.dev news query: "<name>" strike OR fire OR recall OR sanction OR insolvency OR "port closure".
  • Per footprint key (shared across all suppliers): one monitor per port authority, regional notice page or sanctions list page. One incident then fans out to every affected supplier.
  • Noise control:
    • Skip changes that are only layout or timestamps.
    • Gemini must return a verbatim quote.
    • A news item is attributed to a company only on a domain match, or on a name match plus a matching region.
    • Confirmed means an official or primary source, or two independent domains within 48 hours. Everything else stays Unconfirmed and is never pushed to the client.
  • Latency: every event stores t_source → t_webhook → t_classified → t_alert. The screen shows each stage as a chip and the p50/p95 over recent events.
  • Accuracy: a funnel from raw items to classified, quote verified, corroborated and alerted, plus precision and recall on a labelled set of about 40 items. Be upfront that we wrote that set ourselves.

6. Upstream and downstream

Upstream: in, narrow

Two hops through Cala ownership, plus any tier-2 rows the client gives us. It feeds checks C3 and C5, which is where it earns its keep. Upstream companies get news monitors only.

Downstream: stretch, about 4 hours

A "Customer watch" lane that reuses the same pipeline: news monitors on the client's customers for insolvency, restructuring and layoffs. Show coverage honestly ("signal on 9 of 20 customers"). Public signals on small private customers are thin, so this is the first thing to cut.

7. Demo script (3:30)

  1. 0:00Hook: "A supplier posts a notice. How long until you know, and where do you buy instead?"
  2. 0:20Onboarding, live: paste 10 real supplier names. The clock runs, rows go from pending to monitored, and a judge clicks the chooser on one ambiguous name.
  3. 0:55Open a supplier: plants and ports from its own site, each with a source URL, and its monitors armed.
  4. 1:15The live moment: a presenter publishes a "port strike" notice on our labelled test page from a phone. The timer runs, the webhook chip lights up, the classifier card appears with the quote, and the timer stops when the alert renders. Real web changes stream in the feed alongside it.
  5. 2:00The incident: the footprint (port, region) lights up the 3 affected suppliers.
  6. 2:20Alternatives: three candidates. One fails C2 (same port), one is unverified on C5, and one passes all checks plus the live news check, with evidence links. Approve.
  7. 3:00Latency histogram, accuracy funnel, then a second client logs in and sees none of the first client's data. Close on the five criteria.

Backup video recorded by hour 34. If webhooks fail at the venue, the same pipeline polls with a Context.dev scrape every 30–60 seconds, and the chip honestly reads "poll".

Cut in this order:

  1. Downstream lane.
  2. Valio order.
  3. Exa Websets (keep Exa search).
  4. Cala beyond ownership.
  5. Upstream beyond 1 hop.
  6. PDF parsing.

Never cut: the live webhook moment, the check table, the source URLs, and the approval step.
