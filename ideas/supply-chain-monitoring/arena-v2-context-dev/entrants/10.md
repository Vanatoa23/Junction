# Entrant 10: Chokepoint (lens: geography and footprint)
## 0. Bold bet (2 sentences)
Every incident is a geographic footprint (area, corridor or facility point) tied to graph nodes, so "is this alternative hit by the same incident" becomes a visible spatial-plus-graph test on a map. Context.dev monitors and news search detect the change live, and the map with its source URLs is the product.

## 1. Summary and name
- **Chokepoint**: a live map of a client's supply network that turns web-detected incidents into footprints and only offers alternatives that are provably outside them.
- Client enters suppliers (CSV or paste). We enrich, geocode sites, watch every company, and draw incidents as polygons (strike region, flood zone, sanctioned area) or corridors (port closure, shipping lane buffer).
- Context.dev is core: company enrichment, site extraction via structured JSON, monitors with webhooks on supplier pages, and news search for incident discovery.
- Hard rule enforced by a 4-row disjointness check (Section 6).

## 2. Architecture and DB
- **Stack:** TypeScript monolith, Next.js UI with MapLibre GL (OSM tiles, GeoJSON layers). Hosted on Aiven Runtime (A).
- **DB:** Aiven Postgres. Footprints stored as GeoJSON in JSONB; spatial tests via PostGIS if Aiven enables it (A), otherwise turf.js in the API. Postgres also serves as the job queue (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), so no Valkey needed for the demo.
- **Flow:** Context.dev monitor or news search → webhook `/hooks/cd` → raw event stored with `published_at`, `received_at` → Gemini extraction (type, place names, severity, sanction tags) → geocode → incident draft with footprint → recompute affected set against each client graph → alert → alternatives agent → approval queue.
- **Seeded reference data (labelled SEEDED):** ~40 major ports, ~12 shipping corridors as lines with buffers, ~30 countries with sanction tags. Built from public knowledge in 2 hours by one engineer; not presented as live data.

## 3. Data model and incident footprint
- `client` (id), `client_edge` (client_id, from_company, to_company, tier) — private, every query filtered by `client_id` from session.
- `company` (id, name, domain, cala_id, exa_id, cd_enriched_at) — shared, no client data.
- `site` (id, company_id, kind: plant|warehouse|port, point, country, port_code, source_url, geocode_conf).
- `corridor` (id, name, line GeoJSON, buffer_km, port_a, port_b) — SEEDED.
- `incident` (id, type: strike|flood|closure|sanction|conflict, severity, detected_at, published_at, status: draft|confirmed, confidence, source_urls[]).
- `footprint` (incident_id, geom GeoJSON, port_codes[], corridor_ids[], sanction_tags[], upstream_site_ids[] (optional)).
- `alert` (id, client_id, incident_id, affected_company_id, path JSON, created_at).
- `alternative` (id, alert_id, candidate_company_id, check JSONB, rank, status: proposed|rejected|approved).

**Disjointness check (explainable, fail-closed).** For failed supplier S and candidate A under incident I, A passes only if all four rows pass:
1. **Spatial:** no A site inside I's polygon (plus buffer); the first 500 km of A's route to the client does not cross I's polygon.
2. **Corridor:** A's route to the client (site → port → corridor → port → client site) shares no corridor or port with I's footprint.
3. **Graph:** no shared upstream node within 3 hops of A and S (client_edge + Cala edges), and no co-located site within 500 m of any I-tagged site (shared plant).
4. **Sanction:** A's country and ownership chain carry no tag in I's sanction set.
Unknown geocode or missing edges give `unverified`, never `pass`. Only `pass` on all four rows can be approved. The check is stored as JSON and rendered as a table.

## 4. Onboarding and bootstrap
- **Input:** CSV with name, country, tier, optional site address. Tier-2 optional list adds edges.
- **Exa:** for names without a domain, Exa search with company category and filters. Websets to enumerate sites for companies with no site list (A).
- **Cala:** resolve each company to a Cala node; pull owners, parent groups and investors to form first-hop graph edges, which feeds the shared-upstream check.
- **Context.dev:** `company` enrichment for domain, HQ and people; structured JSON extraction from each supplier's "locations" or "about" page to yield sites; batch endpoint for bulk (A on batch limits).
- **Geocode:** Gemini proposes coordinates from extracted addresses; a geocoder service is used as a check (A).
- **Timing (A, to measure):** 10 suppliers live in under 2 minutes; 200 suppliers in about 15 minutes, dominated by Context.dev batch and geocoding.

## 5. Monitoring design
Per company, three streams:
1. **Context.dev monitor** on 2–3 supplier pages (news, locations, press). Webhook on change, with diff and source URL.
2. **Context.dev news search** every 15 min per company name and per port or country in its footprint, queries like "strike", "port closure", "flood", "sanctions".
3. **Exa Monitor** per seeded region (a query per corridor and country) for incidents not yet tied to a known supplier.
- **Noise filter:** Gemini classifies each event; drop if no place or no incident type. Dedupe on (type, footprint, 72 h). Mark `confirmed` on two independent sources or one official source (A on source classes).
- **Latency:** each event stamps `published_at`, `received_at`, `classified_at`, `alert_at`. Dashboard shows p50 and p95 per stage; target webhook-to-alert under 60 seconds (A).
- **Upstream:** in, one hop, using Cala-derived edges and tier-2 lists; monitored with the same streams. **Downstream:** out of the demo (one-line stub in the UI).

## 6. Incident-disjoint alternatives agent
- **Sources:** Exa company search (same product category, filters by country, date and domain), Cala companies in the same category, and the client's existing suppliers. Each candidate goes through Context.dev enrichment before ranking.
- **Ranking (among checked candidates only):** capacity fit (client-entered), great-circle distance to client, margin from nearest footprint edge (km), Context.dev enrichment confidence. Weights are a visible slider (A).
- **Validation:** the Section 3 check runs for every candidate. Failing candidates are shown too, greyed, with the failing row named (e.g., "shares Port X with incident").
- **Approval:** a human clicks approve. Approved switch intent is posted to Valio's mock order API only if time allows (secondary).
- **Gemini** drafts the explanation text only; it never decides pass or fail.

## 7. Upstream and downstream monitoring
- **Upstream: in** (one hop). A shared plant is detected by coordinate proximity and by Cala edges, which is the disjointness check's graph row.
- **Downstream: out** for the build. Mentioned in the pitch as a credit-risk extension that reuses the same footprint model.

## 8. Screens and demo script (timed, 3:45)
Screens: (1) client map with supplier pins and corridors; (2) incident detail with footprint polygon, source URLs, latency timeline; (3) alternatives view with affected suppliers in red, candidates in green or grey, and the 4-row check table; (4) onboarding importer with live enrichment log.
- **0:00–0:25** Client map loads (SEEDED client, labelled). Point out corridors and sites.
- **0:25–1:05** Import a new supplier live. Context.dev enrichment fills HQ and sites from its own page, with source URLs.
- **1:05–2:00** Live change: a real monitored supplier or news page changes and the webhook fires. Fallback: a labelled REPLAY of a recorded real webhook. Footprint appears; affected suppliers light up; latency badge shows.
- **2:00–2:50** Alternatives: three candidates. Two fail with named rows (same corridor, shared upstream); one passes on all four rows. Show the table.
- **2:50–3:20** Approve; switch intent recorded.
- **3:20–3:45** Second client logs in: their map is empty of the first client's data. Map criteria to the five judging criteria on the final slide.

## 9. Build plan, risks, cuts
- **Team split:** A ingest and webhooks (Context.dev, Exa, Cala clients); B geo model and disjointness check; C onboarding and Gemini extraction; D map UI, demo, backup video.
- **Hours:** 0–6 schema, seed data, skeleton; 6–18 ingest plus footprint plus map; 18–30 check, alerts, alternatives; 30–36 onboarding polish and latency dashboard; 36–40 demo rehearsal, backup video, freeze.
- **Risks:** Gemini geocoding errors; no live change during the demo; Context.dev webhook delay; seeded corridor graph too coarse.
- **Mitigations:** pre-watch a real page; labelled replay; a manual "confirm footprint" step in the UI; corridor graph kept small and documented.
- **Cut first:** downstream (already out), Valio order API, Websets, people enrichment, PostGIS (turf fallback), Cala depth beyond one hop.

## 10. Biggest weakness (honest)
Footprints are model-drafted from text and a small seeded geography. A wrong polygon or a missing corridor can produce a false "pass", and the check is only as complete as the seeded port and corridor graph. The fail-closed rule limits the damage but cannot remove it, and the demo relies on a real change happening on cue or a labelled replay.
