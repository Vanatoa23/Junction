# Entrant 06: Bookends, supply and customer risk from the live web (lens: Downstream matters)

## 0. Bold bet (2 sentences)
Customer-side risk (credit, demand, insolvency, layoffs, funding) is a first-class lane with its own graph direction, scores and alerts, sitting beside supplier monitoring rather than behind it. We bet that a tool protecting both receivables and inputs scores higher on real-world impact and real-timeness, and that the customer lane visibly exercises more Context.dev monitors during the demo.

## 1. Summary and name
- **Bookends**: the client maps suppliers (upstream, tier 1 required) and customers (downstream, tier 1 required) in one private graph. Context.dev watches every node live, incidents are matched to a footprint, and alternatives are offered only if incident-disjoint.
- Two lanes, **Supply** and **Customers**, share one signal pipeline and one alert inbox but use different signal sets and scores.
- Secondary stack: Valio's mock ordering API, called only after human approval of a switch.

## 2. Architecture and DB
- Frontend: Next.js on Aiven Runtime **(A)**. Graph view with two lanes.
- Backend: one TypeScript service. Context.dev webhook receiver is a route. Jobs use a Postgres queue (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), so no Valkey or Kafka.
- DB: Aiven managed Postgres for everything. JSONB for evidence, `pg_trgm` for fuzzy company names, row-level security on `tenant_id`. Graph queries are at most 3 hops, done with recursive CTEs. ArcadeDB is not used.
- LLM: Gemini turns pages and articles into a fixed JSON signal schema with a confidence field. Rules run first, the LLM second.
- Flow: client list, then resolve (Context.dev enrichment, Cala), then monitors, then webhook, then normalised signal, then score, then alert, then (on incident) footprint match, then alternatives agent, then human approval, then Valio mock order.
- Tenancy: `companies` is a global cache of public facts keyed by domain. Everything about a client's relationships is tenant-scoped. Monitors are deduplicated per URL, so 300 clients sharing one supplier cost one monitor.

## 3. Data model and incident footprint
- `companies(id, domain, name, country, public_facts jsonb, source_urls[], enriched_at)`: global.
- `nodes(tenant_id, company_id, role supplier|customer, tier, spend_share, notes)`: private.
- `edges(tenant_id, from_id, to_id, kind supplies|buys_from|shares_investor|depends_on_port|shares_plant, source_url, verified)`: private.
- `signals(id, company_id, lane supply|credit|demand|funding, type, published_at, detected_at, source_url, quote, confidence, dedupe_key)`: global facts, with tenant-scoped reads.
- `scores(tenant_id, node_id, axis, value, computed_at, reasons jsonb)`: decays over 90 days.
- `incidents(id, type strike|sanction|port|plant|insolvency, footprint jsonb, detected_at, sources[])`.
- `alerts(id, tenant_id, node_id, incident_id, kind, status new|ack|approved|rejected, evidence jsonb)`.
- `alternatives(id, tenant_id, incident_id, for_node_id, candidate_company_id, disjoint_check jsonb, rank, status)`.
- Footprint facets: region, facility IDs, port IDs, sanction list IDs, parent group, product category. Derived per incident from the source article or page.

## 4. Onboarding and bootstrap
- Input: paste or CSV with name, domain (optional), role, tier, spend share. Tier 1 is mandatory.
- Step 1, about 1 min: Context.dev company enrichment resolves domain, country and description. Unresolved rows go to a review queue.
- Step 2, about 2 min: Cala lookup for funding rounds and investors per company. Shared investors become `shares_investor` edges **(A)**: an imperfect proxy for common ownership, used in the disjoint check.
- Step 3, background: Exa search per supplier category to pre-fetch candidate alternatives. Exa news search per customer for a baseline.
- Step 4: create Context.dev monitors and webhook subscriptions. Exa Monitors cover news queries.
- Timing: about 50 companies end to end in roughly 8 minutes **(A, rate-limit dependent)**.
- Demo data: 40 seeded companies labelled SYNTHETIC in the UI, plus about 6 real public companies.

## 5. Monitoring design
- Supply: Context.dev news search for strike, plant fire, sanction and port closure. Monitors on newsroom and notice pages. Exa Monitor on company name news.
- Customer credit: news for insolvency, administration and restructuring. Monitors on investor and newsroom pages. Cala funding checks daily.
- Customer demand: monitors on pricing and product pages (discontinuation or price changes). Careers page job-count drop of more than 30% in 30 days triggers a signal **(A)**.
- Customer layoffs: news for layoffs or redundancies, plus the careers delta.
- Filings: Context.dev scraping of registry pages only where the client supplied the URL **(A: registry availability varies by country)**.
- Noise filter: (1) dedupe on normalised URL and content hash; (2) a signal needs an allowlisted source or two independent sources; (3) confidence below 0.6 goes to an inbox, not an alert; (4) 24h cooldown per node per signal type.
- Latency: each webhook carries `detected_at`. We store `published_at` from the page or article. We show webhook-to-alert and published-to-alert p50 and p95 on a panel. Target: under 10 s webhook-to-alert **(A)**.

## 6. Incident-disjoint alternatives agent
- Check is deterministic code. The LLM only drafts explanations. A candidate passes only if every facet is disjoint from the incident footprint:
  - region differs from the incident region;
  - no plant or facility in the footprint;
  - no port in the footprint;
  - not matched on any sanction list in the incident;
  - no shared parent group or investor with any affected node.
- The UI shows the table per candidate. Failures read as "rejected: shares Port X" or "rejected: shared investor with supplier A". Rejected candidates are visible, not silently dropped.
- Ranking among passing candidates: category fit, country distance as a lead-time proxy, candidate credit score, and disjoint margin.
- Validation: Context.dev scrapes the candidate's site and news search confirms it is operating **(A)**.
- Human approval is required before the Valio mock order.
- Customer lane: the same footprint check finds customers in the incident region. It raises an alert with a suggested action, such as shorter payment terms or a credit-insurance review. It queues for approval and never offers alternatives.

## 7. Upstream and downstream
- Upstream: in, tier 2 optional, two hops max in the demo. Uses the same monitor pipeline.
- Downstream: in, first-class. Tier 1 customers are required for scoring.
- Join: a customer that is also a supplier, or shares a supplier with another client node, is flagged as shared exposure. This connects both lanes in one view.

## 8. Screens and demo script (timed)
- Screens: onboarding paste with live resolve progress; two-lane graph coloured by score; node page with signals and source URLs; incident page with footprint and disjoint table; alerts inbox with approve.
- 0:00-0:25: a supplier strike and a customer insolvency hit the same quarter.
- 0:25-0:55: paste 46 companies, watch enrichment and Cala investor edges appear.
- 0:55-1:30: live change. A monitored customer newsroom page we control is edited; the webhook fires; the alert shows source URL, timestamp and latency. Labelled "seeded page, real monitor". Covers real-timeness and technical execution.
- 1:30-2:10: a customer's demand and credit score moves from green to amber, with reasons and URLs. Covers impact and creative live-web use.
- 2:10-2:50: strike in region R. Footprint computed; 3 candidates, 2 rejected with reasons, 1 approved; approval fires the Valio mock order. Covers demo completeness.
- 2:50-3:20: latency panel (p95 webhook-to-alert), privacy note (tenant-scoped rows), close.

## 9. Build plan, risks, cuts
- Hours 0-6: schema, tenancy, paste onboarding, Context.dev enrichment.
- Hours 6-14: monitors, webhook receiver, signal normalisation, Gemini extraction.
- Hours 14-22: supply and customer scoring, alerts, graph view.
- Hours 22-30: footprint, disjoint check, Exa alternatives.
- Hours 30-36: Cala investor edges, Valio mock order, latency panel.
- Hours 36-40: seeded data, three rehearsals, backup video.
- Split: E1 backend and DB; E2 monitors and webhooks; E3 frontend graph; E4 agent, disjoint check and demo data.
- Risks: monitors not firing in demo time (mitigate with the controlled page plus a real monitor); Context.dev rate limits; thin Cala coverage for small customers; LLM invents facts (mitigate: quote required, rules first).
- Cut first: Valio order, filings, tier-2 upstream, Exa Websets (use search), animations.

## 10. Biggest weakness (honest)
Credit signals on small private customers are thin: public news and careers pages are sparse, so the customer lane will often show "unknown" rather than real risk. We would show coverage as a visible metric rather than hide it, but a judge may find the customer scores less convincing than the supply ones.
