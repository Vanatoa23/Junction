# e25: The map is the beauty

## 1. Concept
A pale, quiet atlas where your company sits at the centre and everything is calm until something matters. Colour appears only where risk does, so one glance tells a non-expert where the trouble is.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three places, named in plain words, in a top bar: **Map** (home), **Suppliers**, **Alerts**. The Map is what people see first, with a single alert line above it when something is wrong. Suppliers is a plain list with the same status words as the map. Alerts is a calm list of what happened. The presenter's "Test a disruption" control is a small, grey link in the bottom-right corner of the Map, outside the normal flow.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
A panel slides in from the right over the map, so the map never disappears. Order:
1. **Name** (the only required field, auto-focused).
2. **Add more details** as a row of quiet chips: Website, Country, City, What they supply, How critical, Yearly spend, Lead time, Contact, Notes. Each opens one small field when tapped. Nothing is shown until asked.
3. **Save**, which is enabled as soon as a name is typed.

A "Paste a list" tab accepts one name per line. After saving, one line appears: "We are looking for more facts. We will tell you what we found and what we could not."

## 4. Map
- **Base:** a desaturated, light tile style (greys and soft sand), with country names faint.
- **Hub:** the company is a dark ink dot (#1F2933) with its name. Only direct supplier lines leave it: 1 px lines in #AEB8C2.
- **Markers:** 12 px dots coloured by risk: low #2F7D6D, medium #C97A12, high #C2412D. Lines to high-risk suppliers turn red and thicken to 2 px.
- **Zoomed out:** suppliers within one country merge into a numbered bubble, coloured by the worst risk inside it.
- **Zoomed in:** each supplier shows its name beside its dot, and the city is written under it.
- **Click:** a card opens on the right with name, status, the score and its reasons (see section 5).
- **Disruption:** a soft red circle (15% opacity) spreads over the affected place, such as Rotterdam. Affected suppliers pulse once, then settle. The hub line reads: "Why: a port closure at Rotterdam affects 3 of your suppliers."

## 5. Risk and explanation
A score appears as a large number with a word beside it: "High risk, 72 of 100". Under it, up to three reasons in plain sentences, such as "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A small line below says what we could not check, such as "Owner details not found." No percentages of confidence, no model names.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
**Alerts** is a short list, newest first. Each row: a headline ("Port closure at Rotterdam"), place, number of suppliers affected, how serious ("High"), and "Noticed 3 minutes after it was published."

Tapping an alert opens **Alternatives** in the right panel: three ranked cards, each with one badge (**Safe to approve**, **Needs a check**, **Ruled out**), one sentence of reason, and one button. Ruled-out cards are muted and still show why. The approve moment is one primary button, "Approve this replacement." It records the decision, shows "Decision recorded today" beside the supplier, and changes nothing else.

## 7. Visual style
- **Palette:** map base #E9EDEA and land #F4F1EA; surface #FAFAF7; ink #1F2933; secondary text #616E7C; hairline #E4E7EB; hub #1F2933; low and safe #2F7D6D; medium and needs-check #C97A12; high #C2412D.
- **Typography:** Inter. Body 15 px, labels 13 px, headings 22 px, score 48 px.
- **Spacing:** 8 px grid, 24 px panel padding, 16 px between cards.
- **Mood:** an atlas on good paper. Light only, because a light map reads calmer on a stage screen and keeps the red meaningful.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
- Dark mode, login, settings, and any profile screens.
- Drawing deeper tiers (suppliers' suppliers, ports, plants, owners). They feed the score only.
- Charts, dashboards, or KPI tiles. The map and one number carry the story.
- Any technical word, provider name, or internal state label.
- Animated globes, sound, and decorative icons.
- Multiple accent colours. Only the three risk colours and the hub carry meaning.
