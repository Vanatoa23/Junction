## Concept
A calm, map-first monitor that a presenter can walk through as one story: add a supplier, watch it get checked, see a disruption land, choose a replacement, approve it. The feeling is quiet control, never alarm.

## Navigation and screens
Three screens, named in plain words: **Suppliers** (default: map on the left, supplier list on the right), **Alerts** (a short list of what is happening), and **Decisions** (approved replacements). The top bar shows the company name and one button, **Add supplier**. A small **Presenter** toggle in the top bar opens a discreet card, bottom-right, titled **Show me around**, with Next and Back. The card dims nothing; it marks the element being discussed with a soft ring.

## Add-supplier flow
A side sheet with one required field, **Name**. Below it, a quiet link, **Add more details (optional)**, expands into four short groups: Where (website, country, city), What (what they supply, how critical: Low, Medium, High), Terms (yearly spend, lead time), and Contact and notes. A **Paste a list** tab takes one name per line. After saving, the supplier appears on the map with a **Checking...** chip. A line underneath reads what was found and what could not be found.

## Map
The company is a dark-blue hub at the centre, labelled with its name. Each direct supplier is a circle joined to the hub by a thin grey line; nothing deeper is drawn. Zoomed out, circles merge into country counts. Zoomed in, circles show the supplier name and city. Clicking a supplier opens a panel with its status, risk level and plain reasons. A disruption appears as a soft amber area around Rotterdam. Affected circles and their lines turn amber, and the hub caption reads "Port of Rotterdam closure affects 3 suppliers."

## Risk and explanation
Each supplier shows a level word (Low, Medium, High) and a thin bar for the 0 to 100 score. Beneath, up to three reasons as full sentences, for example "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." No formulas, no technical labels.

## Alerts and alternatives
The Alerts list leads with one card: "Port of Rotterdam closed by strike. Affects 3 of your suppliers. Noticed 40 seconds ago." Opening it shows the affected supplier with three ranked alternatives as rows, each with a status word and one sentence: green **Safe to approve**, amber **Needs a check**, grey **Ruled out**. The approve moment is a single button, **Approve this replacement**, followed by "Approved by you, just now." The row moves to Decisions. No second dialog.

## Visual style
Light mode on a warm off-white background. Palette: background #F7F6F2, surface #FFFFFF, ink #1F2A36, secondary text #5B6775, hub #1F4E79, safe #2E7D5B, needs check #B7791F, ruled out #7A8491, disruption #D9822B (map area and affected lines only). Typography: system sans (Inter if available), 16px body, 28px headings, 1.5 line height. Spacing on an 8px grid, 16px card padding, 12px radius. Mood: editorial and calm.

## What you deliberately leave out
No login, settings, or dark mode. No deeper supply tiers on the map. No numeric scores beyond the level bar. No chat, drag-and-drop, export, or outside notifications. No red alarm styling. The presenter card is the only place demo controls appear, and it stays hidden until the Presenter toggle is switched on.
