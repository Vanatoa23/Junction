# e28: Wow in Ten Seconds

## Concept
The first screen is a calm world map with the company at its centre. The moment a disruption lands, the port pulses red and every supplier line it touches visibly reacts, so "are we affected?" is answered before anyone reads a word.

## Navigation and screens
Two screens and one overlay. **Map** is the home screen: full-bleed map, a headline strip at the top, and a "Suppliers" side panel. **Alerts** slides in from the top-right, listing what is happening. **Supplier** opens as a card when a marker or row is clicked. A small "Demo notice" button sits bottom-right in grey, for the presenter only.

## Add-supplier flow
One card, one required field: **Company name**. Beneath it, four quiet chips: "Website", "Location", "What they supply", "More details". Each expands in place and stays collapsed by default. Saving drops a grey, pulsing marker onto the map immediately. A "Paste a list" option accepts one name per line. After saving, a "What we found" block lists the facts found and a plain line such as "Could not find yearly spend."

## Map
- **Hub:** a dark navy (#14213D) ring at the company location, slowly breathing.
- **Lines:** 1.5px #8FA3B8 arcs from the hub to direct suppliers only.
- **Markers:** 14px circles coloured by status. Zoomed out (below country scale), markers merge into numbered country bubbles. Zoomed in, supplier names and city labels appear.
- **Click:** the marker's card shows name, city, status, risk level and reasons.
- **Disruption:** the affected port gets a red ring that pulses three times, then rests. Affected lines turn red and carry a slow dashed flow toward the hub. The hub caption reads "Why: Port of Rotterdam closed." Reduced-motion users get a static ring.

## Risk and explanation
A single badge per supplier: a ring with the score and a word, "Low", "Medium" or "High risk". Beneath it, up to three reasons written as full sentences, such as "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Each reason ends with a small "Source" link. No numbers beyond the score.

## Alerts and alternatives
The alert card leads with a plain sentence: "Rotterdam port closed at 09:40. We noticed 3 minutes later." Below it, affected suppliers in a short list. Alternatives appear as three ranked cards with a coloured tag: "Safe to approve" (teal), "Needs a check" (amber), or "Ruled out" (grey, collapsed). Each has one reason sentence. The approve moment is a full-width button, "Approve this supplier." On click, the card turns into "Approved today, 10:02" and a teal line redraws from the hub to the new supplier.

## Visual style
- **Palette:** background #F6F7F9, text #14213D, secondary text #5B6B7F, ready #2E7D6B, checking #8FA3B8, medium risk #D9A441, high risk #C8102E, ruled out #6B7280, action blue #1F5FBF.
- **Typography:** Inter. Headline 28px semibold, body 16px, labels 13px uppercase at 0.06em tracking.
- **Spacing:** 8px grid, 24px card padding, 12px radius.
- **Mood:** calm, editorial, like a quiet newsroom. Light mode only.

## What I deliberately leave out
Login and settings. Drawn tiers beyond direct suppliers. Any technical term, provider name or status code. Charts, timelines and export. Dark mode. A phone layout. Live search or filters. Animated map transitions beyond the disruption pulse.
