# e29: Two-level zoom story

## 1. Concept
A calm world map that explains itself: from far away you see where your supply comes from, and as you zoom in, each supplier and its road appears. Problems are never hidden in a crowd.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three screens, named in plain words:
- **Overview**: the map on the left (two-thirds), "What is happening" list on the right.
- **Suppliers**: a simple table with name, place, how much they matter, and a risk badge.
- **Supplier detail**: opens as a side panel from either screen. Shows why the risk is what it is, and "Find replacements".

A small "Demo" button sits bottom-right on every screen. It opens a one-field panel for the presenter.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
A single panel titled "Add a supplier". Name is the only required field, with the cursor already in it. Below: Country and City on one row, then "What they supply". Everything else sits behind one line: "Add more details (optional)": website, how critical, yearly spend, lead time, contact, notes. A "Paste a list" tab accepts one name per line.
After saving, a quiet status line lists what the system found and what it could not, for example "Found: website, country. Not found: yearly spend."

## 4. Map
**Hub and lines.** The company is a dark navy pin at its own address. Each direct supplier is connected by one thin grey line. Deeper tiers are never drawn.

**Three zoom levels, one rule.**
- **Far (continents and countries):** suppliers in the same country merge into one round bubble with a count. The line to it gets thicker with more suppliers.
- **Middle (regions):** bubbles split into city bubbles, merging only where pins would overlap (under 40 px apart).
- **Close (cities and roads):** each supplier is its own pin with its name, and the lines follow real roads.

**Clustering rule.** Pins merge only when they are closer than 40 px on screen. A cluster is coloured by its worst supplier.

**Affected suppliers never cluster.** They always appear as individual pins with a slow pulse, so an alert is visible at every zoom.

**Click.** A bubble zooms to its area. A pin opens the supplier panel.

**Disruption.** The Rotterdam incident shows as a red ring around the port. The hub's caption reads: "Port of Rotterdam closed since 06:40. 3 of your suppliers are affected."

## 5. Risk and explanation
A risk level (Low, Medium, High) with a number underneath. Under it, up to three plain sentences, for example "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Each reason has a small icon (ship, factory, warning) and a "Where?" link that zooms the map to it.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
The alert list shows one card per event: what happened, where, which suppliers, how serious, and "Noticed 4 minutes ago". Cards are sorted by seriousness, then time.
Replacements appear as a ranked list of three. Each row has a status word, "Safe to approve", "Needs a check" or "Ruled out", a one-sentence reason, and an "Approve" button. Approving asks "Use [name] instead of [old supplier]?" with Yes and Cancel, then shows "Approved today by you".

## 7. Visual style
- Background: `#F6F5F1`. Panels: `#FFFFFF`. Text: `#1C2430`. Muted text: `#6B7280`.
- Hub: `#1F3A5F`. Lines: `#B8BEC7`. Cluster fill: `#DCE6F0`.
- Low: `#2E7D5B`. Medium: `#C98A1B`. High: `#B23A3A`.
- Font: Inter, 16 px body, 28 px headings. Spacing on an 8 px grid.
- Light only. Calm and quiet; colour appears only where something needs attention.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
- Deeper supply tiers, ports and plants drawn on the map (they only feed the risk score).
- Login, settings and user roles.
- Charts, graphs and dashboards of past data.
- Technical labels, provider names and status codes.
- Dark mode and mobile layouts (the stage screen comes first).
