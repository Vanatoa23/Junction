# e22: Large type, open space, one idea per screen

## 1. Concept
A calm printed page that says one true thing at a time, with one thing to do. The feeling is steady control, never alarm.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three screens, reached by three large words at the bottom: **Today**, **Suppliers**, **Map**. The app opens on Today, with one sentence such as "All 10 suppliers are running normally." or "A closure at Rotterdam affects 3 suppliers." No sidebar, no menus. The presenter's control is a small grey word, **Demo**, top right. It opens one panel with one button: "Publish a notice".

## 3. Add-supplier flow
One question per screen, large field, large placeholder. Order: **Name** (the only required step), **Country**, **City**, **What they supply**. Then one quiet line, "Add more details", which reveals the rest only when tapped. For a list, one large text box: "One supplier per line." After saving, one screen states what we found and what we could not find, as two short lists.

## 4. Map
Full-screen map with muted tiles. The company is one large dot at the centre, labelled with its name. Direct suppliers are small dots joined by thin grey lines. No labels when zoomed out; city names appear only at local zoom. Clicking a dot opens one card: name, one sentence on status, one button, "See options" or "Why". A disruption shows as affected dots pulsing once, their lines turning red, and the company dot carrying one sentence: "3 suppliers are affected by the Rotterdam closure." Offline, a light grey world outline replaces the tiles.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Each supplier shows one large word, **Low**, **Medium** or **High**, with the score in small type beneath. Below it sits one reason as a sentence: "Two of its suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A "More reasons" link reveals up to three more. No charts, no numbers beyond the score.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
Today lists alerts one at a time, newest first, each a sentence plus "Noticed 12 minutes ago". Tapping opens a full screen with the headline and the affected supplier, and one button: "Show replacements". Replacements appear as at most three large names, each with one badge (**Safe to approve**, **Needs a check**, **Ruled out**) and one reason sentence. Ruled-out options are dimmed and not tappable. Approval is one large button, "Approve this supplier". The confirmation reads "Approved on 14 November. Your team has been told." No second dialog.

## 7. Visual style
Light only. Background `#F7F6F3`, ink `#1C1B19`, secondary text `#6B6862`, hairlines `#E4E1DA`. Safe `#2F5D50`, needs a check `#B5651D`, high risk and disruption `#A33A2E`, ruled out `#9A9892`. One sans-serif family (Inter, falling back to the system UI). Headlines 56px at weight 600, line height 1.05. Body 22px, line height 1.5. Labels 16px uppercase, letter spacing 0.06em. Spacing on an 8px base, screen margins 96px on desktop, text column no wider than 720px. The mood is a printed page, not a dashboard.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
Charts, tables, filters, search, settings, login, dark mode, tooltips, notifications, and any supplier tier beyond the first. No status jargon, no icons beyond one dot and one arrow. Every explanation is a sentence, never a control.
