# e21: Zero-config demo mode

## 1. Concept
The app opens already alive: ten European suppliers on a calm map, one amber warning at Rotterdam, nothing to set up. Adding a supplier feels like writing a line in a notebook: the name lands on the map as you press Enter, and findings arrive as quiet ticks.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Two places in a top bar: "Today" (opens first) and "Suppliers". A small "Run demo alert" control sits at the far right, dimmed, for the presenter. Alternatives open as a side panel from an alert, never a separate screen.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
The Suppliers list starts with one line, "Add a supplier", with the name field focused. Enter saves it; the new row appears and its dot pulses on the map. Under the saved row, three quiet chips offer "Add website", "Add city", "Add yearly spend". Each expands in place into one field; ignoring them costs nothing. Paste mode accepts one name per line and ticks each off as it resolves. Under each supplier, one plain line reports the result: "Found: website, city" or "Could not find a city. Add one?"

## 4. Map
The company is a dark ink dot at the centre, labelled with its name. Lines are thin grey curves, one per direct supplier only. Markers are small circles, sized by yearly spend when known. Zoomed out, nearby markers merge into a count per country; zoomed in, names appear. Clicking a marker opens a card with name, city, risk level in words, and "See why". A disruption turns the affected supplier and its line amber, adds a soft amber ring around the hub, and gently frames the incident. Offline, a pale outline of Europe replaces tiles.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Lead with the level word ("Low", "Medium", "High") and one calm bar; the number 0 to 100 sits small beside it. Reasons are at most three plain sentences stated as facts: "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Anything unchecked reads "Not checked yet", never a guess.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
Today shows a short list, newest first. Each row: what happened ("Port of Rotterdam closed"), what it touches ("3 suppliers, 1 critical"), and when we noticed ("Noticed 4 minutes after it was reported"). Opening an alert shows three ranked replacements as cards tagged "Safe to approve" (green), "Needs a check" (amber) or "Ruled out" (grey), each with one sentence. The approve moment is one large button, "Approve this replacement". After the click it becomes "Approved today" with an "Undo" link for ten seconds.

## 7. Visual style
Light and paper-like. Background #F7F6F2, surface #FFFFFF, ink #1C2430, muted text #5B6573, lines #C9CED6, hub #1C2430, safe #2E7D5B, check #B7791F, ruled out #8A8F98, high risk #B4442E. Inter throughout: 15px body, 22px section titles, 32px only for the greeting. Spacing on an 8px grid. Map tiles desaturated to match the paper tone.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
No login, accounts or settings page. No charts or dashboards. No deeper tiers drawn. No provider names, status codes or confidence modes. No tour: one line at the top reads "Example data: ten suppliers in Europe." No dark mode.
