# Round r1-m13: e25 vs e26

## Winner: e25 "The map is the beauty"

e25 wins because it is calmer and more legible at every zoom level. Its colour rule is strict: only the three risk colours and the hub carry meaning, so the red disruption reads at a glance. e26 reuses amber for both the disruption area and the "Needs a check" badge, which blurs the one signal a non-technical reader most needs to trust. e25 also specifies zoom behaviour more precisely (merged bubbles coloured by the worst risk inside, names and cities when zoomed in), and it keeps the add-supplier chips and the "what we could not check" line honest without adding screens. Both are feasible in a day, with e26 slightly heavier (a fourth "Decisions" screen and a presenter tour).

## Best idea of the loser worth stealing (e26)

The "Show me around" presenter card: a Next/Back walkthrough that marks each element being discussed with a soft ring, rather than only a single trigger button. On stage it turns the demo into one told story (add, check, disruption, choose, approve), which e25's lone "Test a disruption" link does not offer.

## Scores (priority order)

1. Non-technical understanding in seconds: e25 (fewer screens, the alert line sits above the map, the hub caption explains the disruption).
2. Calm, polished look: e25 (single accent system, no colour overlap).
3. Map explains itself at both zooms, direct lines only: e25 (more precise zoom and colour rules). Tie on direct-only lines.
4. Simple add-supplier flow: e25, narrowly (name first, nine optional chips, nothing shown until asked).
5. Alerts and alternatives, clear and honest: e25, narrowly (explicit "could not check" line).
6. Feasibility in one day: roughly tied, e26 slightly heavier.
