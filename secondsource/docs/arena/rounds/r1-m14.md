# Round 1, judge m14: e27 vs e28

**Winner: e28 (Wow in Ten Seconds)**

**Why:** e28 gives a non-technical reader the answer in seconds: a calm full-bleed map, a headline strip, and a hub caption that states "Why: Port of Rotterdam closed." Its alert wording ("We noticed 3 minutes later") and its one-field add-supplier card with quiet expandable chips are simple and honest. Its editorial palette and card system look more polished and considered than e27's austere, checklist-style treatment, and the map behaviour (country bubbles when zoomed out, names when zoomed in, arcs to direct suppliers only) is buildable in one day of Leaflet work.

**Criteria check (priority order):**
1. Understood in seconds: e28 edges it, with the headline strip and the "Why:" caption on the map. e27 is also clear but needs the reader to move between three screens.
2. Calm and polished: e28 has the more finished visual identity (ring badges, 12px radius, editorial type). e27 is calm but reads as a rule set more than a designed look.
3. Map explains itself: both keep lines to direct suppliers only. e28 explains the disruption in place through the caption. e27's labelled hatched area is a good idea but adds build cost.
4. Add-supplier flow: e28's single required field and four collapsed chips are lighter than e27's five visible fields plus a disclosure.
5. Alerts and alternatives: both are honest. e28 adds a Source link on each reason, which strengthens trust.
6. Feasibility: both are buildable. e28's pulse and flowing dashed lines need some CSS/SVG work but stay within a day, with reduced-motion handled.

**Best idea of the loser worth stealing (e27):** risk shown as a word plus a distinct shape (Low = circle, Medium = triangle, High = square with heavy outline), so the level never depends on colour alone. Fold this into e28's risk badge, which currently leans on red, amber and teal.
