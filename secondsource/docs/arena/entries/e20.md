## 1. Concept
Every disruption arrives as a message in an inbox, the way email does: first it is new, then it is handled. The map stays beside the inbox as context, so the message says what happened and the map shows where.

## 2. Navigation and screens
One working screen with three named areas: **Inbox** (left, default), **Suppliers** (a list with an Add button), and **Map** (right, always visible). Supply and Sales see the same inbox, filtered by two chips: "Affects materials" and "Affects deliveries". A small "Send test notice" button sits in the footer for the presenter.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
A single sheet with one required field: **Name**. Below it, "Add more details (optional)" opens four short groups: Where (country, city), What they supply, How important (Critical, Important, Minor), and Terms (yearly spend, lead time, contact, notes). A "Paste a list" tab takes one name per line. If the name looks like a web address, the sheet fills country and city from it. After saving, one calm line reports: "Found 6 facts. Could not find: lead time." The supplier appears in the list at once.

## 4. Map
The company is a dark-blue hub in the centre. Thin grey lines run to direct suppliers only. Zoomed out, suppliers merge into country counts; zoomed in, each shows its name. Grey means normal, amber means medium risk, red means affected by an open incident. Clicking a supplier opens its card beside the map. An open incident gets a soft red area, affected lines turn red, and the hub label reads "Why: port closure at Rotterdam". Clicking an inbox message flies the map to that incident.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Each supplier shows a level word (Low, Medium, High) and a thin score bar. Beneath, up to three reasons appear as plain sentences: "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A "Why this score?" link reveals the rest. No formulas, no confidence labels.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
An inbox row shows a blue dot while new, a plain title ("Strike at Port of Rotterdam"), the affected supplier names, and "Noticed 4 minutes after it was posted." Opening a message shows what happened and a "Find replacements" button. Replacements form a short ranked list, each tagged **Safe to approve** (green), **Needs a check** (amber) or **Ruled out** (grey, with a reason). Approving is one button, "Use this supplier", followed by "Recorded today by you." The message then moves to Handled.

## 7. Visual style
Light and calm. Background #F6F7F9, surface #FFFFFF, text #1C2330, muted text #5B6575, hub #1F4E79, neutral lines #B8C0CC, safe #2E7D5B, check #B7791F, ruled out #7A8291, high risk #B42318. Font: Inter, 16px body, 20px headings, 13px labels. Spacing on an 8px grid; inbox rows 72px tall. Mood: a quiet mail app with one map.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
No login, no settings page, no deeper supplier tiers drawn, no raw source links on the main screen, no confidence labels, no provider names, no charts or dashboards, no dark mode, no search box, and no phone layout beyond tablet.
