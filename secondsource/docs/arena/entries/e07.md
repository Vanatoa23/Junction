# e07: Conversational add

## 1. Concept
One calm box sits at the top of every screen. Type "we buy cartons from Nordpack in Germany" and the supplier appears on the map, with the company's questions answered in plain sentences. It feels like briefing a sensible assistant who listens, asks one thing at a time, and shows its work.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three screens, named in plain words: **Overview** (map and alert list, the opening screen), **Suppliers** (a simple list with status), and **Alert** (one event, its affected suppliers and replacements). The top bar holds the company name, the add box, and a quiet "Demo" button for the presenter at the far right.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
1. The user types a sentence or pastes a list. The box reads out what it understood as small chips: "Nordpack", "Germany", "Cartons". Each chip can be tapped and corrected.
2. Only the name is required. If the country or city is missing, one follow-up question appears under the box with a "Skip" link. Spend, lead time, contact and notes are never asked; they sit behind a collapsed "More details".
3. Pasted lists become one row per line with a single button: "Add 6 suppliers".
4. After saving, a "What we found" panel lists what the search added and what it could not, for example: "Found: website, port of shipment. Could not find: yearly spend."

## 4. Map
- **Hub:** the company is a dark navy dot at the centre of the map.
- **Direct suppliers:** teal dots joined to the hub by thin grey lines. No deeper tiers are drawn.
- **Zoomed out:** nearby dots merge into a numbered cluster; lines stay curved and light.
- **Zoomed in (city level):** dots show names and lines become straight.
- **Click:** a card slides in from the right with name, status, the top risk reason, and "See alternatives" if affected.
- **Disruption:** affected dots and their lines turn amber or red, and the line draws toward the incident point. The hub caption states the cause in one sentence: "Two suppliers are affected by the Port of Rotterdam closure."
- **Offline fallback:** a plain light-grey outline of Europe with the same markers.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Show the level as a word with a number: "High, 78". Below it, at most three reasons written as full sentences, strongest first: "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Each reason has a small "Why?" that reveals the source and date. Never show formulas or decimals. Where nothing is known, say so honestly: "No information yet about its owner."

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
The Overview alert list has one row per event: "Port of Rotterdam closed · 2 suppliers affected · noticed 3 minutes ago". The Alert screen shows a two-sentence summary, the affected suppliers, and ranked replacement cards, each tagged "Safe to approve" (green), "Needs a check" (amber) or "Ruled out" (grey, with the reason). Each card has one sentence of reasoning and one strong button: "Approve this replacement". Approval needs no confirmation dialog; the card then reads "Approved by you, 14 Nov" and the header shows "1 of 2 decided".

## 7. Visual style
Light and calm, built for a projector.
- Background `#F6F7F4`, surface `#FFFFFF`, border `#E2E6E1`
- Text `#1C2A2E`, secondary text `#5A6A6F`
- Hub `#1F3A4D`, supplier `#2A8C8C`, line `#B8C2BE`
- Safe `#2E7D5B`, needs check `#B7791F`, ruled out `#8A949A`, high risk `#B42318`
- Typography: Inter, 16px body, 13px labels, 28px page titles
- Spacing on an 8px grid; cards 12px radius, no shadows except on the slide-in card
- Motion: one line-draw on disruption, nothing else

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
No login, settings page, or dashboards of charts. No deeper-tier markers or lines on the map. No technical status words, and no bulk file upload (paste only). No free-text search across the map. No animation beyond the single disruption line-draw. No confirmation dialog for approvals.
