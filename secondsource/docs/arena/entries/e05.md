## 1. Concept
A five-question setup where the map draws itself as you answer: your company at the center, each supplier a line that appears as you name it. It feels like a short conversation that ends in a picture you already understand.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three top tabs in plain words: **Map**, **Alerts**, **Suppliers**. The first visit opens a full-screen setup, then the Map. A quiet **Show a notice** button sits bottom right for the presenter. Replacements open in a side panel, not a new page.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
One question per screen, large type, a visible **Skip** every time.
1. Name (required). **Paste a list** accepts one name per line.
2. Country, then city or website.
3. What they supply.
4. How critical: "Stops us if late", "Hurts if late", "Easy to replace".
5. Yearly spend and lead time.

Contact and notes live under **More details** on the Suppliers page. After each supplier, a **What we found** card lists facts found, and **We could not confirm** in grey.

## 4. Map
The hub is a navy circle with the company name. Each direct supplier gets one thin line, drawn in when created. Markers are sized by criticality: neutral when fine, amber when they need a choice, red when disrupted. Zoomed out, nearby markers merge into country bubbles showing a count; zoomed in, names appear. Clicking a supplier opens its side card. A disruption adds a pulsing ring at the incident, turns affected lines amber, and a banner at the hub says why: "Port of Rotterdam closed. 2 suppliers ship through it."

## 5. Risk and explanation
Each supplier shows one level word (Low, Medium, High) with a colored dot, then up to three full-sentence reasons: "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Each reason carries a grey source line, such as "public news, 3 days ago." Unchecked facts come last, in grey: "We could not check their own suppliers." The 0 to 100 score appears only in a tooltip.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
Alerts show a plain headline ("Port of Rotterdam closed by strike") and "Affects 2 suppliers, noticed 40 seconds after publication." The replacement panel shows up to three ranked cards, each with a badge: **Safe to approve** (green), **Needs a check** (amber), or **Ruled out** (grey, collapsed). Each card has one sentence of reason. **Use this supplier** is one click, records the decision with today's date, and shows no confirmation dialog.

## 7. Visual style
Light and calm. Background #F7F8FA, surface #FFFFFF, text #1F2933, muted text #667085, hub #1E3A5F, neutral lines and markers #94A3B8, safe #2F855A, needs check #B7791F, disrupted #C05621, ruled out #6B7280. Inter throughout: 28px headings, 16px body, 14px labels. 8px spacing grid, 12px card radius, 1px #E4E7EC borders, no shadows. Lines draw in over 300ms; the disruption ring pulses every 2 seconds.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
Login and accounts, several companies, deeper tiers drawn on the map, provider or technical names, score charts, a settings page, dark mode, a mobile layout, and a live news ticker.
