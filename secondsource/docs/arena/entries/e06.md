# e06: Spreadsheet-familiar

## 1. Concept
Adding a supplier feels like typing a new row in a sheet people already know, while the map beside it redraws as each row lands. The feeling is calm and orderly: every supplier is a row, and every row shows where it sits and what it is connected to.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three plain screens, switched by a top bar with two labels: **Suppliers** (default) and **Alerts**. Both keep the map on the right half.
1. **Suppliers**: the sheet on the left, the map on the right.
2. **Alerts**: a short list on the left. Opening an alert shows its replacements in a sheet that slides in on the left.
3. **Show a live incident**: a small grey button bottom-right, for the presenter only.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
The sheet has four visible columns in this order: Name, Country, City, What they supply. Only Name is required. Criticality (Low, Medium, High), Yearly spend, Lead time and "+ more" (website, contact, address, notes) sit behind a "More columns" toggle, so the sheet stays calm. Empty cells show a faint grey hint, not a form label.
- Tab moves across cells; Enter starts the next row.
- Paste a list from a spreadsheet or plain text and the rows are created at once, with a line above the sheet: "Read 12 suppliers. 2 need a country."
- After saving, a row shows "Checking", then "Ready". A line under the row says what was found ("Found 4 facts") and what could not be ("Could not find lead time").

## 4. Map
- **Hub**: a dark navy circle at the centre, labelled with the company name.
- **Lines**: thin grey lines from the hub to each direct supplier only. No deeper tiers.
- **Markers**: small circles, coloured by risk level.
- **Zoom out**: nearby suppliers merge into a count ("3 suppliers"). **Zoom in**: each marker shows its name and city.
- **Click a marker**: a small card with name, status, risk level and the first reason. "Open row" jumps to the sheet.
- **Disruption**: an incident pin pulses once. Affected suppliers turn amber or red, and their lines follow. The hub shows one sentence: "Port of Rotterdam closure affects 3 suppliers." The map eases to the incident.
- **Offline**: a neutral grey world outline.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Each row has a single word (Low, Medium, High) and a thin bar for the 0 to 100 score. The word is the answer; the bar is secondary. Clicking "Why" opens up to three plain sentences, most important first, such as "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." The last line reads "Looked at 14 sources", so a non-expert can see the work was real.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
Alerts are newest first. Each shows a headline ("Port of Rotterdam: dock strike"), the place, the number of suppliers affected, and "Affecting you since 14 Nov, 09:10". That answers both Head of Sales questions in one line.
The replacement sheet has columns Supplier, Region, Why, and Status. Status is a chip: **Safe to approve** (green), **Needs a check** (amber), or **Ruled out** (grey, with the reason). Ruled-out rows cannot be approved. Each approvable row has one **Approve** button. After one click the row reads "Approved by you, 14 Nov, 09:42", and that record stays visible.

## 7. Visual style
Light, quiet, ledger-like.
- Background #F7F7F5; surface #FFFFFF; grid lines #E3E3DF
- Text #1F2328; muted text #6B6F76
- Hub #1F3A5F; neutral supplier #5B7083
- Medium risk #C98A1B; high risk #C2412D
- Safe #2E7D4F; needs a check #B7791F; ruled out #8A8F98
- Type: Inter or the system sans. Sheet text 14px, labels 13px, headings 20px.
- Rows 36px tall; 8px spacing grid; 1px lines, no shadows except the open card.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
Login, settings, charts and dashboards, drag-to-edit on the map, and tier diagrams. No technical words, provider names, or status codes. No sending of messages or emails. Animation is limited to the single incident pulse.
