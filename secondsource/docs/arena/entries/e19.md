## 1. Concept
A calm control room: suppliers on the left, the map in the middle, and details that slide in from the right only when asked. Nothing jumps, so a non-technical person always knows where they are.

## 2. Navigation and screens
One screen, no login. Top bar: company name, a plain status line ("Watching 10 suppliers. Checked 2 min ago"), an "Alerts" button with a count, and "Add supplier". Left column: supplier list grouped "Needs your choice", "Affected", "Everything else"; each row shows name, city, a risk dot and a one-line status. Right drawer opens for a supplier, an alert or the add form. The presenter's "Demo" button is small and grey, bottom right.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
The drawer opens with one field, "Supplier name", and a blue "Add" button. Below sits a collapsed "Add more details (optional)" section, in this order: website, country, city, what they supply, how critical (Low / Medium / High), yearly spend, lead time, contact, notes. "Paste a list" accepts one name per line. After saving, the drawer shows "Checking now", the facts found so far with ticks, and "We could not find: ..." for gaps.

## 4. Map
Light grey base map. The company is a dark teal hub labelled with its name. Thin grey lines run to direct suppliers only; dots are coloured by risk. Zoomed out, nearby dots cluster with a count; zoomed in, names appear. Clicking a row or dot pans there and opens the drawer. A disruption draws a soft amber circle labelled "Port of Rotterdam: strike"; affected lines and dots turn amber or red and pulse once, and the hub caption reads "2 suppliers affected". Offline fallback: plain world outline with the same dots.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Show the level word first ("High risk"), then the 0 to 100 number in small text. Beneath, up to three plain sentences, most important first: "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A footer reads "Based on public news, sanctions lists and company records." A score never appears without its level and reasons.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
An alert shows a plain headline, "Noticed 4 minutes after it was reported", and affected suppliers as chips. Alternatives appear as three ranked cards, each with name, city, a tag ("Safe to approve" green, "Needs a check" amber, "Ruled out" grey) and one sentence of reason. Ruled-out options sit under a collapsed "Show ruled-out options". Each safe card has one "Approve replacement" button. After one click it reads "Approved, 14 Nov, 10:42" with a single "Undo" link. Nothing changes without that click.

## 7. Visual style
Light, calm, editorial. Background #F7F8F6, surface #FFFFFF, primary text #1E2A28, secondary text #5B6B68, hub #0F6E68, lines #B8C2BF, borders #E1E6E3, low risk #2E7D5B, medium #B7791F, high #B5402E, action blue #1F5FA8. Font Inter: 15px body, 13px secondary, 20px drawer titles. 8px spacing grid, 24px drawer padding, 56px list rows.

## 8. What we deliberately leave out
Dark mode, login, settings, charts and dashboards, supplier tiers beyond direct suppliers, technical status words, separate supplier pages, animation beyond one pulse, export, and a tablet-specific layout.
