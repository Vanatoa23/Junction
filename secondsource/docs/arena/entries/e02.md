# e02: Executive dashboard

## 1. Concept
One calm screen answers "are we affected, how badly, since when" before anyone scrolls. Big status cards carry the answer; the map and a ranked list show where and what to do next.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Two views, no login. The top bar holds the wordmark "SecondSource", "Suppliers", "Add supplier", and a discreet "Presenter" control at the far right. The default view is "Overview": status cards on top, map and "Needs attention" list below. Clicking a supplier opens a side panel with its details, reasons and replacements. Nothing else.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
A dialog with one required field, "Supplier name". "Add" activates once it is filled. Below, a quiet disclosure "Add details (optional)" reveals website, country, city, what they supply, criticality (Low / Normal / Critical), yearly spend, lead time, contact and notes, in that order. A "Paste a list" tab takes one name per line. After saving, a line says "Checking public news and records" and then lists what was found and what could not be found, in plain words.

## 4. Map
A Leaflet map fills the lower left, about 60% width. The company is a dark navy hub labelled "You". Thin grey lines run from the hub to each direct supplier only. Markers are 12px circles coloured by status. Zoomed out, nearby markers cluster and show the worst status colour. Zoomed in, names appear beside markers. Clicking a marker highlights its line and opens its panel. During a disruption, a pulsing ring marks the incident (Port of Rotterdam), affected lines and markers turn red, and the hub shows one caption: "3 of your suppliers ship through this port." Offline, the world is a light grey outline with the same markers.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Each supplier shows a score from 0 to 100, a level word ("Low", "Medium", "High") and a bar. Up to three reasons appear as short sentences, such as "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A "Why this score?" link expands the rest. No weights, decimals or source names beyond "news reports" and "company records."

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
Four status cards sit across the top: "Suppliers affected", "Highest risk", "Deliveries at risk" (with "since 14:20") and "Last checked". Below the map, "Needs attention" lists each affected supplier with one line of what happened and "noticed 4 min ago". Opening a supplier shows up to three replacements as cards, each with a chip: "Safe to approve" (green), "Needs a check" (amber), or "Ruled out" (hidden until requested). Each card carries one sentence of reason. The approve button is full width and reads "Approve this replacement". It records the decision with a time stamp and marks the row "Replaced".

## 7. Visual style
Light theme. Background #F6F7F9, surface #FFFFFF, border #E3E7ED, text #1B2430, secondary text #5B6677. Hub #1F3A5F, normal supplier #7A8794, medium #E0A21B, high #C8462E, safe #2E7D5B, check #B7791F. Typography: Inter. Body 15px, card figures 40px semibold, labels 13px uppercase with 0.04em tracking. Spacing on an 8px grid, 24px card padding, 16px gaps. Cards have 12px radius and a 1px border with a faint shadow. No gradients, no animation beyond one pulse per change.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
Login, settings, charts over time, deeper supplier tiers on the map, weights and decimals in scores, a separate alerts page, search and filters, dark mode, export, notifications, and any technical label or provider name. Mobile stacks the cards and list vertically and that is all.
