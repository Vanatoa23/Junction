# e08: Risk score is the hero

## Concept
Every supplier is a circle on the map, sized and coloured by its risk, with one plain line beside it saying why. Glancing at the map answers "where is the trouble, and what is it?"

## Navigation and screens
Three screens, named in plain words: **Suppliers** (map and list, the home screen), **Alerts** (a short, calm feed), and **Add supplier** (a side sheet opened from the map). An alert opens **Replacements**. The top bar shows the company name and "Checked 2 min ago". At the far right, a small grey "Show a test notice" control serves the presenter.

## Add-supplier flow
One visible field: **Supplier name**. Pressing Enter adds the supplier to the map at once, with status "Checking". Below it, a quiet link, "Add details for a better picture", opens optional fields in this order: Website, Country, City, What they supply, How critical (Low, Medium, High), Yearly spend, Lead time, Contact, Notes. Nothing else is required. "Paste a list" accepts one name per line. After the search, a panel shows "What we found", each fact with its source, and "We could not find", each with one retry link.

## Map
Muted, greyscale base map. The hub is a dark ink circle labelled "Your company" at the centre. Each direct supplier has one thin grey line to the hub; the line takes its supplier's colour at 60 percent opacity when risk is medium or high. Circles scale with score: 14 px at low risk, up to 36 px at high. Colours: low `#2E7D6B`, medium `#C98A1B`, high `#B23A3A`.
- **Zoomed out (continent):** circles merge into country totals showing the count and the highest colour in that country.
- **Zoomed in (city):** each circle shows its name and its one-line reason.
- **Click:** a card slides in from the right with the score, the level word, up to three reasons, and "See replacements" only when affected.
- **Disruption:** affected circles pulse once, then hold a ring. Their lines turn red. The hub carries a label such as "Port of Rotterdam closed: 3 suppliers affected", and the map frames the incident and those suppliers.

## Risk and explanation
The score appears large in the card (for example "72") with the level word beneath: Low (0 to 39), Medium (40 to 69), High (70 and above). Reasons are full sentences that start with the place: "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Show at most three, strongest first. A footer reads "From public news and records, updated 4 min ago." Never show a number without its level word.

## Alerts and alternatives
**Alerts** is a vertical list, newest first. Each card holds a plain title ("Strike at Port of Rotterdam"), the affected supplier names with colour dots, a severity word, and "Noticed 3 minutes after publication".
**Replacements** lists up to four options ranked by score. Each has a status pill, "Safe to approve" (green), "Needs a check" (amber), or "Ruled out" (grey, collapsed by default), plus one sentence of reason. The top option has one large button, "Approve replacement". After approval, the line reads "Approved 14 Nov. Recorded." and the button disappears.

## Visual style
Palette: background `#F7F6F3`, surface `#FFFFFF`, ink `#1E2430`, secondary text `#5B6270`, lines `#C9CDD4`, low `#2E7D6B`, medium `#C98A1B`, high `#B23A3A`. Typography: Inter, 16 px body, 13 px labels, 32 px score numbers. Spacing on an 8 px grid, 16 px card padding, 12 px radius. Mood: calm and editorial. Light only.

## What you deliberately leave out
Second-tier lines and map markers for ports, plants or owners; any numeric weights or formulas; confidence or mode settings; provider names; progress bars and job status; history charts; dark mode; login; a settings screen.
