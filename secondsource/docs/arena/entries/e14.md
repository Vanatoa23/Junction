# e14: Editorial / newspaper

## 1. Concept
SecondSource reads like a well-edited daily paper. Each disruption is a short news story and the map is its figure, so the feeling is informed calm: a crisis made readable before the first coffee.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three screens under a masthead with the company name and today's date: **Today** (map and lead story), **Suppliers** (list and add form), **Alerts** (earlier stories). The presenter's "Run a demo" link sits quietly in the footer.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
A slip-form panel opens from "Add a supplier". Name is the only required field and gets focus. Below it, "Add details (optional)" expands into quiet one-line fields in this order: Website, Country, City, What they supply, How critical (Low / Medium / High), Yearly spend, Lead time in days, Contact, Notes. No helper text. A "Paste a list" tab takes one name per line. On save, "What we found" lists facts with their sources, and "Could not find" states gaps plainly, for example "Yearly spend: not public".

## 4. Map
Leaflet with greyscale tiles washed in paper colour; the offline fallback is the world outline in ink hairline. The company is a solid ink dot labelled "Our company" in serif. Lines are 1px ink hairlines with a slight curve, direct suppliers only. Markers are small squares, like type: ready green, medium risk amber, high risk brick, checking grey.
Zoomed out, each country shows one square with a count. Zoomed in, city and supplier names appear. A click opens a photo-caption card under the map: name, city, level, one reason sentence.
A disruption draws a halftone hatched circle around the incident. Affected lines turn brick and thicken, and the hub caption reads "3 suppliers affected by the Rotterdam port closure."

## 5. Risk and explanation
Level word first in small caps (Low, Medium, High), then "score 72 of 100" in grey, then a thin rule as a bar. Reasons are written as a reader would see them in print: "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Show at most three, newest first, each with its source in grey: "Harbour authority notice, 14 Nov." No charts.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
An alert is a news item: brick kicker in small caps ("PORT DISRUPTION"), a serif headline of ten words or fewer ("Rotterdam port closed for 48 hours"), a one-sentence standfirst, then the map as a 16:9 figure captioned "Figure 1. Affected suppliers, Rotterdam area." Byline: "Noticed 4 minutes after publication."
Replacements sit below under a "Replacements" rule, ranked, three rows maximum. Each row has a tag ("Safe to approve" in green, "Needs a check" in amber, "Ruled out" in grey), the supplier name in serif, and one sentence of reason. The approve moment is one black button, "Approve replacement." It turns into a printed stamp, "Approved by you, 14 Nov, 10:42," and the supplier's status updates. No confirmation dialog.

## 7. Visual style
Light only. Paper #F7F4EC for background, ink #1B1B1B for text and the hub, secondary text #5E5A52, hairline rules #D6D0C2, brick #A23B2A for alerts and high risk, green #2F5D50 for safe and ready, amber #B7791F for check and medium, grey #8A857A for checking. Headlines in Source Serif 4 semibold (34/40px); body in Source Sans 3 (16/24px); kickers 12px uppercase with 0.08em tracking. 8px spacing grid, 24px column gutters. Sections separated by rules, not cards. Mood: broadsheet, quiet, generous margins.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
Dark mode, notifications and sound, login and settings, charts or number dashboards, deeper tiers drawn on the map, badges and emoji, technical words such as source names or job states, and any animation beyond one fade on a new alert.
