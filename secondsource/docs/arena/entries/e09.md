## 1. Concept

An incident is told as one short story in five beats, from "we noticed" to "you approved a replacement." The feeling is a calm colleague briefing you in under a minute: clear, paced, never alarming.

## 2. Navigation and screens

Three screens, labelled in plain words in one top bar: **Today**, **Suppliers**, **Replacements**. The app opens on Today: one headline sentence ("Port of Rotterdam is closed. 3 of your suppliers are affected.") above the story, with the map beside it on desktop. Suppliers holds the list and the add flow. Replacements opens from a beat in the story. A small grey "Publish a test notice" button sits bottom-right for the presenter.

## 3. Add-supplier flow

The first screen of Suppliers shows one field: "Supplier name", plus Add. Nothing else is required. Once saved, the supplier card appears with the status "Checking public news..." Below it, quiet chips offer the optional facts: Website, Country, City, What they supply, How critical, Yearly spend, Lead time, Contact, Notes. Clicking a chip reveals one field. "Paste a list" accepts one name per line. Results read as two short lines: "Found: country, website, 2 facts." and "Could not find: yearly spend."

## 4. Map

The company is a dark filled circle at the centre, labelled with its name. Each direct supplier is a white dot joined to the hub by a thin grey line. No deeper tiers are drawn.

- Zoomed out (continents): dots merge into numbered clusters; lines curve gently.
- Zoomed in (one region): dots become labelled pins with straight lines.
- Click a supplier: a side card slides in with name, city, status, risk level and reason.
- Disruption: a single soft ring pulses at Rotterdam. Affected suppliers and their lines turn coral. The hub line reads "Why: Port of Rotterdam closed."
- Offline fallback: a plain grey world outline with the same dots and lines.

## 5. Risk and explanation

Each supplier shows the level as words first ("High risk"), with the number 0 to 100 small beside it. Colour supports the words and never carries meaning alone. Reasons appear as at most three plain sentences, strongest first: "2 of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A quiet line underneath reads "Based on 14 public checks, updated 3 minutes ago." A "How we know" link lists sources in everyday language.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives

The alert is the story itself, a vertical timeline of five beats, each with a time and one sentence: **We noticed**, **We checked**, **Suppliers affected**, **Replacements found**, **You approved**. The current beat is highlighted.

Replacements show three ranked cards, each with a label and one-sentence reason: "Safe to approve" (green), "Needs a check" (amber), "Ruled out" (grey, collapsed by default). The approve moment is one button, "Approve this replacement." After one click, beat five fills: "Approved by you at 10:42. Recorded."

## 7. Visual style

Light only, editorial and calm, like a morning briefing.

- Paper background #F6F5F1, surface #FFFFFF, line #D9D6CE
- Ink #1E2A32, muted text #6B7780
- Hub #1E3A46, safe #2F7D5B, check #B7791F, high risk #C2553D
- Type: Inter. Headline 28px, body 16px, timestamps in tabular numerals.
- Spacing on an 8px grid; cards 24px padding, 12px radius.
- One motion only: the pulse at the incident.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out

Dark mode, login, settings, export, email or push notifications, chat, and any deeper-tier lines on the map. No filters or sorting beyond search on the supplier list. No technical words, no provider names, no raw data feeds, and no charts other than the map.
