# e31: Card-based supplier profiles

## 1. Concept
Every supplier is a calm, readable card, like a business card that also shows whether it is in trouble. The feeling is "I can see my whole supply base at a glance and know who needs me today."

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three screens, named in plain words:
1. **Overview**: the map fills the left 60 percent, supplier cards sit in a scrolling column on the right, and a short alerts strip runs across the top.
2. **Supplier**: a full card with its risk history, reasons and replacement options.
3. **Add supplier**: one panel that slides over the map.

The top bar has "Suppliers" and "Alerts". A small "Demo: publish a test notice" button sits in the corner for the presenter.

## 3. Add-supplier flow
One panel, one scroll. Order: Name (required, focused on open), then "Where are they?" (website, country, city), then "What do they supply?". Everything else sits behind one link, "Add more details (optional)", which reveals criticality as three large chips (Critical, Important, Nice to have), yearly spend, lead time, contact and notes. A "Paste a list" tab accepts one name per line. After saving, the card says "We are checking this supplier now", then lists what was found and what could not be found.

## 4. Map
The hub is a dark solid circle labelled "Your company". Thin 1.5px curves run from the hub to direct suppliers only. Each marker is the supplier's initial in a coloured circle: grey for low risk, amber for medium, red for high. Zoomed out, markers cluster by continent with a count. Zoomed in to city level, each marker shows the supplier name. Clicking a marker highlights its card in the column and opens a small preview.

A disruption shows as a pulsing ring at the location (Port of Rotterdam), the affected lines turn red and dashed, and the hub carries one line: "Port of Rotterdam closed. 3 suppliers affected."

## 5. Risk and explanation
Each card has a risk dial: a 240-degree gauge from 0 to 100, the number in its centre and "Low", "Medium" or "High" beneath. Under the dial, a small "Risk over 30 days" line chart has a dot for today. Below that, up to three reasons in plain sentences, such as "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." A score never appears without its reasons.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
The alerts list is at the top of Overview, one row per event with five labelled parts: What happened, Where, Which suppliers, How serious, Noticed (for example "4 minutes ago"). An affected card gets a red band: "Affected by Port of Rotterdam closure".

On the Supplier screen, alternatives are three rows, each with a status pill: "Safe to approve" (green), "Needs a check" (amber) or "Ruled out" (grey, collapsed by default). Each row has one sentence of reason and one "Approve" button. After approval the row reads "Approved on 14 Nov" and the decision is recorded.

## 7. Visual style
Light theme for the stage screen. Palette: background #F7F6F2, surface #FFFFFF, border #E3E1DA, text #1C1F26, secondary text #5B6270, hub #1F3A5F, low risk #2E7D5B, medium risk #C98A12, high risk #B83A3A, map lines #9AA3B2. Typography: Inter, 16px body, 28px card titles, 40px risk number. Spacing on an 8px grid, 24px card padding, 16px corner radius, 1px border, no heavy shadows. Mood: quiet, editorial, like a well-set newspaper page.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
Deeper tiers drawn on the map, dark mode, login, charts beyond the single risk line, animated tickers, emoji, drag-and-drop sorting, a settings page, and any technical or provider names in the interface.
