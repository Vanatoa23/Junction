# e17: Weather-forecast metaphor

## 1. Concept
SecondSource reads supply risk like a weather forecast: most suppliers sit under clear skies, and a storm warning appears only when something real is coming their way. The feeling is calm and informed, like checking the forecast before you leave.

## 2. Navigation and screens
Three tabs top left, plain words: **Overview**, **Suppliers**, **Alerts**. Overview opens first: the map fills two thirds of the screen, and a "Today" panel sits beside it ("9 suppliers under clear skies. 1 storm warning.").
Three screens plus a supplier side card. A small "Demo" button sits bottom right and opens one action: "Send a storm warning".

## 3. Add-supplier flow
The Suppliers screen opens with one large field, "Supplier name", and an Add button. Pressing Enter adds the supplier at once.
Below it, a quiet link, "Add details (optional)", expands to fields in this order: Website, Country, City, What they supply, How critical (Low, Medium, High as segmented buttons), Yearly spend, Lead time in days, Contact, Notes. Nothing beyond the name is required.
A "Paste a list" link opens a box for one name per line.
After saving, the row reads "Checking the forecast..." and then shows two short lists: "Found" (for example "country, website, 2 port facts") and "Couldn't find" (for example "owner").

## 4. Map
- **Hub:** one deep-blue dot labelled "Your company", with a soft halo.
- **Lines:** thin, from the hub to each direct supplier only. Colour follows the weather: Clear teal, Advisory amber, Storm red.
- **Zoomed out (continents):** nearby suppliers merge into a bubble showing a count. The bubble takes the worst colour inside it.
- **Zoomed in (region, city):** individual markers with supplier names.
- **Click a marker:** a side card slides in with the name, the weather word, one reason sentence, and "See alternatives".
- **Disruption:** one slow translucent red ring around the incident, labelled "Port of Rotterdam: storm warning". Affected lines and markers turn red. The hub shows a caption: "3 links under storm warning." Nothing flashes.

## 5. Risk and explanation
Three words only: Clear, Advisory, Storm, mapped from low, medium and high. The score sits small beside the word ("Storm, 78 of 100").
Reasons read like a forecast, one line each: "Two of its own suppliers ship through Rotterdam." Show three; "See all 5 reasons" expands the rest.

## 6. Alerts and alternatives
The Alerts screen is a vertical list, newest first. Each card gives a headline in plain words ("Strike at Port of Rotterdam"), the place, the suppliers affected, and "Noticed 40 seconds after it was reported".
Opening an alert shows three ranked replacements, each with a chip: "Clear to approve" (teal), "Check first" (amber), or "Ruled out" (grey, with its reason, not selectable). Each row carries a name, city, one reason sentence, and an Approve button.
Approve takes one click, with no dialog. The row then reads "Approved by you, 14 Nov, 10:42", and the supplier moves to Clear.

## 7. Visual style
Light and calm, like a clear morning.
- Sky background #F3F7FA; surface #FFFFFF; border #DCE4EC
- Text #1B2A3A; muted text #5B6B7C
- Hub #2F5D8C
- Clear #2E9E8F; Advisory #D9A21B; Storm #C8452F; Ruled out #9AA5B1
- Typography: Inter, with system-ui fallback. Body 16 px, labels 14 px, headings 28 px at weight 600.
- Spacing: 8 px grid, 24 px panel padding, 16 px corner radius.
- Light mode only. No gradients except the storm ring.

## 8. What you deliberately leave out
- Tiers beyond direct suppliers, drawn or listed.
- Login, settings, exports, charts, and any technical term.
- Dark mode, sound, pop-ups, flashing, and animated radar maps.
- Email or push notifications.
- Confirmation dialogs on Approve.
